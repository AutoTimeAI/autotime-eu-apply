# AutoTime European Tech Mobility Market R&D

> **Evidence status: Phase 0 hypothesis map.** This document is an initial portfolio synthesis, not
> the evidence-deep R&D package, legal validation, or production authority. Country support requires
> the claim-level source ledger, route dossiers, counter-evidence review, test corpus and expert
> sign-off defined below. See `evidence-deep-rd-2026-09/README.md` for the active research programme.

**Research date:** 10 September 2026  
**Coverage:** 20 European markets for non-EU technology professionals  
**Foundation:** AutoTime EU Apply Core Product Investment Strategy and Startup Moat R&D Dossier  
**Decision purpose:** choose where, how and under what evidentiary controls AutoTime should expand.

## Founder research principle

> **Deepness provides clarity, and clarity makes me clever.**

This is a company operating principle, not a slogan. Research is complete only when it improves a
business or product decision, makes uncertainty visible, and defines what evidence would change the
decision. Country coverage is not “complete” because a page exists. It is complete when official
sources, rules, inputs, outputs, exceptions, change monitoring, test cases, expert review and safe
failure behaviour all exist.

## Executive decision

AutoTime should not launch 20 apparently equal country products. It should build **one governed
European decision platform** and release country packs in evidence-based waves:

1. **Production beachhead — Germany and Netherlands.** These combine strong tech relevance with
   comparatively structured eligibility inputs. The Netherlands also supplies an official recognised
   sponsor register; Germany supplies explicit Blue Card salary thresholds and shortage/new-entrant
   treatment.[^1][^2]
2. **Validated expansion — Ireland, France, Sweden and Spain.** These add commercially useful
   corridors and meaningfully different rule shapes: occupation lists, annually referenced salary,
   qualification-or-experience tests, and employer-initiated processes.[^3][^4][^5][^6]
3. **Controlled expansion — Austria, Denmark, Finland, Belgium, Luxembourg, Portugal and Estonia.**
   These are valuable but add points systems, regional administration, multiple parallel schemes,
   smaller addressable pools or operational-source complications.
4. **Research/watchlist — Poland, Czechia, Italy and Lithuania.** Build source registries and test
   corpora first; do not expose definitive personalised recommendations until current national rules
   and implementation practice have expert sign-off.
5. **Separate products/boundaries — United Kingdom, Switzerland and Norway.** They are valuable
   European destinations but not EU Blue Card implementations. The UK also creates a particularly
   important regulated-advice boundary; Switzerland includes quotas and labour-market priority.[^7][^8]

The strategic estimate is **12–18 months to responsibly operate six markets**, and **24–36 months to
operate 15–20 markets at a defensible standard**. A one- or two-hour exercise can produce market
triage and a research backlog. It cannot produce legally reviewed rules, verified change detection,
country test corpora, production monitoring and outcome evidence across Europe.

## What this research establishes—and does not

This report establishes a portfolio thesis, source map, comparative rule model, investment sequence,
architecture and validation programme. It is reliable enough to guide product investment. It is **not
legal advice**, a substitute for national counsel, or authority to market an eligibility result as
certain. Official pages themselves may be summaries; law, subordinate instruments, administrative
practice and candidate facts can change the result.

### Confidence vocabulary

| Level                  | Meaning                                                | Permitted product behaviour      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| R0 discovered          | Relevant official source identified                    | Internal research only           |
| R1 structured          | Rule and inputs transcribed with effective dates       | Prototype with visible caveat    |
| R2 verified            | Two-person review, examples and boundary cases pass    | Private UAT, no definitive claim |
| R3 expert-signed       | Qualified jurisdiction reviewer signs the rule version | Bounded candidate recommendation |
| R4 production-observed | Change monitoring, replay and user outcomes operate    | Marketed availability with SLO   |

No market should be labelled “supported” below R3. Missing or stale inputs must yield **unknown**, not
an optimistic result.

### Ranking interpretation

The best launch market is not simply the biggest. It has a reachable customer segment, official
sources that can be governed, material candidate uncertainty AutoTime can reduce, enough comparable
cases to evaluate, and affordable expert support. Germany/Netherlands is the strongest paired test:
Germany tests complex but explicit route logic; the Netherlands tests authoritative sponsor-entity
matching. Ireland is attractive because its Critical Skills route is occupation-centric. France and
Sweden are good tests of annual salary-reference maintenance. Spain is strategically important but
its 2026 salary implementation and parallel national/Blue Card routes require careful versioning.[^6]

## Country research dossiers

### 1. Germany — production beachhead

Germany's official portal publishes 2026 EU Blue Card thresholds of **€50,700** generally and
**€45,934.20** for shortage occupations and qualifying new entrants.[^1] This makes salary evaluation
highly machine-checkable, but only after AutoTime has classified compensation period/currency,
occupation, graduation timing and qualification/experience evidence.

- **Product value:** explain why a vacancy is above/below threshold, show the decisive evidence and
  compare Blue Card with other skilled-worker possibilities without pretending route selection is
  certain.
- **Required adapters:** annual threshold; shortage occupation taxonomy; recognised/comparable
  qualification workflow; contract duration; regulated-profession flag.
- **Hard cases:** bonuses/equity, part-time roles, ambiguous occupation, foreign qualification
  comparability, recent-graduate date, and vacancy text that omits salary.
- **Release gate:** expert-reviewed rule pack, 50 golden cases, 20 adversarial cases, effective-date
  boundary tests and explicit unknown handling.

### 2. Netherlands — production beachhead

The Highly Skilled Migrant route requires an employment contract with an IND-recognised sponsor,
income requirements and market-rate remuneration.[^2] IND publishes a recognised-sponsor register
and updates it regularly.[^9] That combination creates an unusually valuable product surface:
employer verification plus eligibility evidence rather than generic job matching.

- **Product value:** verified sponsor identity, register timestamp, salary test and contradictions
  between a job ad, employer claims and authoritative data.
- **R&D problem:** entity resolution—trading names, legal names, groups, subsidiaries and ambiguous
  matches. “Likely match” must never render as “verified sponsor.”
- **Release gate:** precision-first employer matcher, exact-source snapshot, monthly refresh plus
  unscheduled change checks, and a manual review queue for non-exact matches.

### 3. Ireland — occupation-led expansion

Ireland's Critical Skills Employment Permit depends on eligible occupation, remuneration,
qualifications and a job offer normally lasting at least two years. Current official guidance states
2026 remuneration bands including **€40,904** for listed occupations with a relevant degree and a
higher all-occupation route subject to exclusions.[^3]

- **Product value:** map a job to the Critical Skills and Ineligible lists with explainable confidence;
  show which title/description facts control the mapping.
- **Primary risk:** titles alone are weak. Use duties and occupation codes, store mapping confidence,
  and request review on conflicting descriptions.
- **Release gate:** version both occupation lists and remuneration schedules; test every list change
  against historical decisions.

### 4. France — threshold and route taxonomy

France's official visa portal describes Talent routes and an EU Blue Card path requiring a contract
of at least one year, qualifying higher education or comparable experience, and a salary of
**€59,373 since 21 August 2025**.[^4]

- **Product value:** distinguish highly qualified employee/Blue Card from innovative-company and
  other Talent paths; avoid a single “Talent Passport eligible” label.
- **Risk:** route names, translations and implementing references can change. Preserve the French
  source wording, English rendering, decree reference and effective date.
- **Release gate:** bilingual legal/product review and route-specific evidence checklist.

### 5. Sweden — annual salary reference

Sweden requires highly qualified employment for at least six months, higher education corresponding
to 180 credits or five years' relevant experience, and salary of at least 1.25 times average gross
salary. The published threshold is **SEK 53,625/month from 15 July 2026**.[^5]

- **Product value:** transparent threshold and completeness review before employer initiation.
- **Risk:** annual threshold dates and employment-condition review; a salary-only screen is
  insufficient.
- **Release gate:** effective-dated threshold tests, education/experience evidence, health-insurance
  prompts only where applicable, and employer workflow explanation.

### 6. Spain — parallel high-qualified routes

Spain exposes a national highly qualified professional route and an EU Blue Card route. The official
information recognises higher education or professional-experience substitutes; for ICT managers and
professionals the Blue Card experience alternative can be three years within the previous seven.
Spain published a 2026 order and subsequent salary-threshold update through its Large Companies and
Strategic Groups Unit.[^6]

- **Product value:** route comparison and evidence readiness, particularly for experienced
  technologists without conventional degrees.
- **Risk:** never infer the applicable salary from an older page whose note says implementation is to
  be supplemented. Resolve the 2026 instrument and capture its exact effective rule before R2.
- **Release gate:** Spanish-language primary-law review, current salary implementation, and employer
  submission/workflow tests.

### 7. Austria — explainable points, not a black-box score

Austria's Red-White-Red system contains multiple categories. Very Highly Qualified Workers can use a
six-month job-seeker visa and need at least 70 points; a matching job offer is required for the card.
The official calculator is explicitly orientation rather than an authority decision.[^10]

- **Product value:** evidence-backed points worksheet and route comparison.
- **Risk:** do not convert self-reported points into eligibility certainty. Each awarded point must
  link to a document and rule version.
- **Release gate:** reproduce official examples, test rounding/category caps, and label formal AMS
  assessment as authoritative.

### 8–9. Denmark and Finland — structured but multi-route

Denmark offers Pay Limit, Positive List and Fast-track routes; employer certification matters for
some tracks. Finland offers specialist and EU Blue Card routes; its official specialist page defines
expert duties requiring special expertise.[^11] These are promising technically but demand a
route-selection layer, not isolated threshold calculators.

- **Product value:** ask the minimum questions necessary to identify plausible routes, then produce a
  document/readiness plan.
- **Release gate:** official scheme and employer-register adapters, current salary/list versions,
  national-language review, and no default route when two remain plausible.

### 10. Belgium — regional governance problem

For work over 90 days, highly qualified staff generally use a single-permit workflow. Work
authorisation does not itself guarantee the federal residence decision, and employment rules and
salary conditions differ by region.[^12]

- **Product value:** determine region before calculating; explain work/residence duality.
- **Risk:** a Belgium-wide rule row is structurally wrong. Model Brussels, Flanders and Wallonia as
  separately versioned authorities sharing a federal residence layer.
- **Release gate:** three regional expert reviews plus federal dependency tests.

### 11. Luxembourg — deterministic but smaller

Luxembourg's official March 2026 guidance requires a highly qualified contract of at least six
months, annual salary of at least **€65,652**, qualifications, and employer declaration of the vacancy
to ADEM.[^13]

- **Product value:** high-confidence checklist and threshold evaluation.
- **Commercial implication:** useful premium corridor and excellent decision-engine test, but likely
  too small to lead acquisition.

### 12. Portugal — useful demand, elevated operational risk

Portugal supports highly qualified activity, EU Blue Card and Tech Visa routes. AIMA's current page
still displays salary reference values tied to 2023/2024 statistics, while describing a partly
digitising administrative process.[^14] That is a strong warning against naïvely presenting the page
as a current calculator.

- **Product value:** document readiness and route navigation first; personalised eligibility later.
- **Release gate:** resolve current legal values, verify Tech Visa employer data, test appointments and
  workflow in practice, and obtain Portuguese expert sign-off.

### 13. Estonia — digital-market candidate, source pack incomplete

Estonia is attractive for its digital and start-up ecosystem and has multiple possible skilled/start-
up pathways. In this research pass, a complete current primary-source rule chain was not established.
It therefore remains **R0**, regardless of commercial attractiveness.

- **Next evidence:** Police and Border Guard Board route rules, salary coefficient/effective values,
  employer obligations, start-up committee dependencies and worked cases.
- **Safe state:** market education only; no candidate-level conclusion.

### 14–17. Poland, Czechia, Italy and Lithuania — research packs

These markets should enter the source registry now, not the production recommender. Poland's official
MOS system supports highly qualified-employment applications.[^15] Czech official materials describe
Blue Card and Employee Card paths.[^16] Italy has updated Blue Card qualification rules to allow
specified professional experience, including specialised technical talent.[^17] Lithuania requires a
fresh primary-source chain in this programme.

Their common issue is not lack of opportunity; it is insufficiently resolved current implementation,
salary/occupation mappings, translations and administrative dependencies. Each needs an R1 rule pack,
20 expert-labelled cases and local review before prioritisation is revisited.

### 18. Norway — separate skilled-worker model

Norway is outside the EU Blue Card system. UDI states that skilled workers normally need completed
higher education or vocational training and a concrete job offer; the job and conditions must satisfy
Norwegian requirements. Employer confirmation is used in relevant cases to reduce fraudulent job
offers.[^7]

- **Product value:** verified job-offer workflow and qualification-to-job match.
- **Risk:** “skilled” is contextual and pay is compared with normal Norwegian conditions, reducing
  pure calculator determinism.

### 19. Switzerland — premium, manual-first

For third-country nationals, Switzerland generally admits highly qualified workers subject to limited
permits. The employer must show no suitable Swiss/EU/EFTA candidate and customary salary/conditions;
cantonal and federal stages matter.[^8]

- **Product value:** explain feasibility constraints and collect a strong employer evidence pack.
- **Safe state:** “requires employer/cantonal assessment,” not eligible/ineligible. Keep manual-first
  until expert-labelled outcomes justify narrower automation.

### 20. United Kingdom — separate regulated-advice track

The Skilled Worker route requires an eligible job with an approved sponsor and the applicable salary
or going rate. The government publishes a licensed-sponsor register.[^18] However, candidate-specific
immigration recommendations may cross the boundary between general information and regulated
immigration advice under IAA guidance.[^19]

- **Product value:** source-linked facts, sponsor verification, data completeness and scenario
  education.
- **Prohibited until written scope:** prescriptive route choice or personalised “you should/qualify”
  output without the required regulatory arrangement.
- **Architecture consequence:** jurisdiction policy must control output verbs, confidence labels,
  human escalation and audit records—not just disclaimers.

## The moat architecture required by the market research

The 20-country comparison validates the ten-capability programme. These are not optional polish:

1. **Versioned mobility sources** — content hash, authority, jurisdiction, retrieval and effective
   dates, language, legal basis, reviewer and supersession link.
2. **Source-change detection** — scheduled retrieval, semantic/structural diff, severity triage,
   dependency graph and release freeze for critical unresolved changes.
3. **Immutable decision records** — append-only input, fact, rule, model and output snapshots.
4. **Employer/sponsor verification** — authoritative register snapshots and conservative entity
   resolution with exact/possible/unverified states.
5. **Candidate evidence provenance** — every consequential fact has source, extraction method,
   confidence, confirmation and expiry.
6. **Claim-to-evidence links** — every application claim maps to evidence spans; unsupported claims
   block export or require explicit review.
7. **Decision replay** — execute old facts under old and new rule versions and explain deltas.
8. **Correction/disagreement capture** — preserve original, correction, actor, reason and resolution;
   never silently overwrite.
9. **Country readiness scores** — derive from source freshness, test coverage, review, monitoring and
   operational health, not a manually chosen marketing status.
10. **Expert sign-off records** — reviewer scope, jurisdiction, credentials/contract reference,
    signed rule version, limitations and expiry.

### Canonical decision contract

Every decision should persist:

```text
decision_id, tenant_id, candidate_snapshot_id, vacancy_snapshot_id
jurisdiction, route_id, rule_bundle_id, evaluated_at, effective_at
facts_used[], evidence_links[], employer_verification_id
result = pass | fail | conditional | unknown | review_required
reasons[], missing_facts[], conflicting_facts[], assumptions[]
allowed_output_policy, reviewer_state, supersedes_decision_id
```

A result without its source bundle and facts is not a decision record; it is disposable UI text.

### Country-pack contract

Each pack must contain: jurisdiction and route identifiers; source inventory; extracted atomic rules;
effective dates; controlled vocabulary mappings; salary/list datasets; evidence requirements;
exceptions; 50+ golden/boundary cases for production markets; output-language policy; expert sign-off;
change cadence; and rollback/replay instructions.

## Reliability and evaluation programme

### Metrics that matter

- **Source freshness SLO:** 100% of critical sources checked on schedule; unresolved critical change
  disables affected conclusions.
- **Decision reproducibility:** 100% of sampled decisions replay to the stored result under the stored
  bundle.
- **Citation completeness:** 100% of decisive reasons link to exact source and rule.
- **Employer precision:** no known false “verified” matches; ambiguous recall loss is preferable to a
  false positive.
- **Abstention quality:** unknown/review_required is expected when inputs or rules are insufficient.
- **Expert agreement:** track agreement by rule, route and severity; do not hide disagreement in an
  aggregate accuracy score.
- **User comprehension:** users can correctly state what the recommendation means, its decisive facts,
  uncertainty and next action.
- **Behavioural outcome:** measure fewer unsuitable applications, better evidence completion and
  application continuation—not unearned “more interviews.”

### Minimum adversarial cases

Test salary absent/ranged/bonus-heavy/hourly/part-time; employer alias/subsidiary/acquisition; degree
missing or unrelated; experience substitute at exact time boundary; occupation-title mismatch;
regulated profession; contract one day short; source changes mid-session; conflicting candidate
documents; revoked sponsor; rules effective after a saved decision; and translated source drift.

## Business model and validation

### Initial customer

The narrowest credible segment is a non-EU software/data/product/security professional applying from
abroad to Germany or the Netherlands, with enough experience to target skilled sponsorship and enough
uncertainty to value route/employer evidence. Do not launch to “anyone seeking work in Europe.”

### Value proposition

> Know which cross-border tech roles are realistically actionable, why, what evidence is missing, and
> whether the employer is verifiably positioned to sponsor—before investing in the application.

### Willingness-to-pay experiments

Run founder-led concierge UAT before broad build-out:

1. Five target users complete ten real vacancy decisions each.
2. Compare unaided decisions with AutoTime-supported decisions.
3. Measure comprehension, changed apply/skip behaviour, time saved and error correction.
4. Offer paid packages rather than hypothetical price questions: e.g. £19 decision pack, £39 monthly
   evidence workspace and £99 human-reviewed corridor pack. These are test prices, not conclusions.
5. Require at least three paid users and two repeat-use events before interpreting demand positively.

Do not estimate TAM by multiplying “all European tech workers” by a subscription price. Build a
bottom-up model from target-origin/destination flows, sponsor-addressable openings, reachable intent,
activation, paid conversion, retention and contribution margin. Label every uncertain factor and run
sensitivity bands.

### Unit economics ledger

Per paid cohort measure payment fees, model cost, source acquisition, expert review amortisation,
support, corrections/refunds and country-operations labour. Contribution margin must be calculated
after these costs. Expert-assisted products may support higher prices, but only if review throughput
and liability/compliance costs are included.

Hiring sequence: mobility research lead; senior data/platform engineer; product engineer for evidence
UX; fractional privacy/security; contracted jurisdiction experts; then source-operations analyst. A
large generic feature team before this spine exists would increase breadth without defensibility.

## Stop, continue and expansion rules

Continue investing when users understand the output, materially change decisions for defensible
reasons, return with new vacancies, pay, and generate corrections/outcomes that improve the governed
system. Pause a country when critical sources cannot be monitored, expert disagreement is unresolved,
false verified employers occur, or support/review cost breaks the economic case. Stop the moat thesis
if users prefer generic application automation, will not pay for decision confidence, or evidence-led
recommendations do not improve behaviour compared with a clear official-links checklist.

## Immediate founder decisions

1. Approve Germany/Netherlands as the only production-track markets for the next core milestone.
2. Approve Ireland/France/Sweden/Spain as R&D packs, not simultaneous launches.
3. Treat the UK as a separate regulated track and Switzerland as manual-first.
4. Fund the ten foundational capabilities before more generic career or application features.
5. Commission scoped written expert reviews using the country-pack contract.
6. Run paid concierge UAT alongside engineering; neither can validate the other alone.
7. Never market “more interviews” until controlled evidence supports it.
