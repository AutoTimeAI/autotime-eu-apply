# AutoTime Evidence-Deep R&D Package

**Status:** Phase 2 evidence-deep desk R&D complete; external validation programme active  
**Started:** 11 September 2026  
**Scope:** European technology-worker application decisions and the defensibility of AutoTime's evidence-backed cross-border decision system.

## Purpose

This package upgrades the preliminary 20-market hypothesis map into evidence that can withstand founder, product, engineering, commercial and expert scrutiny. It does not treat elapsed time or page count as completion evidence.

## Completion boundary

The 2–3 hour research window can complete a strong **desk-research and product-investment package**:

- primary-source market and route register;
- current rule and effective-date comparison;
- competitor and substitute matrix;
- market-priority model with transparent weights;
- moat, counter-moat and build-versus-partner assessment;
- implementation-ready data, governance and evaluation specifications;
- commercial hypotheses, sensitivity ranges and falsification tests;
- contradictions, unknowns and follow-on evidence requirements.

It cannot create evidence that requires external elapsed work:

- target-user interviews or observed application behaviour;
- paid willingness-to-pay and retention results;
- legal or immigration-adviser opinions;
- live production source-monitoring history;
- production incident, deletion, recovery or outcome evidence.

Those are explicitly tracked as validation work, never presented as completed research.

## Research quality gates

A material claim is usable only when its jurisdiction and population are explicit; the source is authoritative; publication, retrieval and effective dates are recorded; supersession and contradiction checks are complete; fact, inference and recommendation are distinguishable; and the product consequence and safe failure state are stated.

For production route logic, an official summary page alone is insufficient. The chain should include the controlling law or instrument, current administrative guidance, referenced amounts/lists, and expert review.

## Evidence established at kickoff

### Market demand is real but not uniformly accelerating

Eurostat reports 10.45 million ICT specialists in the EU in 2025, 5.0% of employment, up 59.4% from 2015. Germany accounted for 2.3 million and France 1.4 million; Sweden, Luxembourg and Finland had the largest ICT-employment shares. This supports a substantial technology-worker context, but not a claim that every market has an acute current shortage.[^1]

The EU information-and-communication vacancy rate was 2.2% in Q4 2025, while the total EU vacancy rate was 2.0%. Vacancy rates had softened from 2024. Therefore “Europe has a tech shortage” is unsuitable as the sole demand thesis; corridor, occupation and employer-level evidence is required.[^2]

### Germany has exceptional revealed Blue Card volume

The EU issued 78,096 Blue Cards in 2024, down 12.3% from 2023. Germany issued 56,252—72.0% of the EU total—while Poland issued 5,853, France 2,775 and Spain 1,924. Sweden, Estonia and Portugal each issued fewer than 100. This materially supports Germany-first and weakens any roadmap that treats nominal Blue Card availability as equal commercial evidence.[^3]

### Netherlands offers a high-value verification primitive

The Dutch Highly Skilled Migrant route requires an IND-recognised sponsor, qualifying income and market-rate salary. For 2026 the published monthly thresholds include €5,942 for candidates aged 30+ and €4,357 below 30; the reduced highly-skilled-migrant threshold is €3,122. The recognised-sponsor register is updated monthly.[^4][^5]

This is a stronger product primitive than a generic employer sponsorship claim, but creates a hard entity-resolution obligation. AutoTime must preserve legal-name/register identity and return “possible match” or “unverified” when a trading name cannot be resolved exactly.

### Competitive space has two flanks

Candidate-side suites compete on breadth and speed. Simplify+ combines autofill, tailoring, writing, networking and tracking at $39.99/month; Teal+ lists $29/month; Huntr Pro lists $40/month; Careerflow Premium lists $23.99/month.[^6][^7][^8][^9] This validates the warning that generic tracking, resume tailoring and autofill are mature bundled categories.

Employer-side mobility providers compete on expertise and case execution. Jobbatical advertises pre-hiring immigration assessment, expert-managed cases and support in 45+ countries; Localyze combines mobility software with experts, case management, integrations and ISO 27001; Deel combines expert visa services with a 75+ country footprint.[^10][^11][^12]

The plausible whitespace is therefore narrow: **candidate-controlled, pre-application, evidence-linked cross-border decision intelligence**. It is not yet proven defensible. Employer-side vendors could move earlier in the funnel, candidate suites could add shallow visa screens, and public infrastructure such as the EU Talent Pool could reduce discovery advantage.[^13]

## Initial implications

1. Keep Germany/Netherlands as the beachhead hypothesis, now supported by different evidence: German revealed route volume and Dutch sponsor verifiability.
2. Do not rank countries mainly by ICT workforce share or the presence of a Blue Card route.
3. Model candidate-side job suites and employer-side mobility platforms as separate competitor sets.
4. Treat public EU infrastructure as both an input and a substitution threat.
5. Test whether candidates pay for avoided low-probability applications—not for another document tool.
6. Make source governance, entity resolution, replay and expert sign-off part of the product itself.
