# Competitive and Counter-Moat Analysis

## Decision

AutoTime should not claim an immigration-calculator moat. Eligibility screening already exists inside employer-side mobility platforms, while candidate-side suites can cheaply add shallow visa questions. The defendable position, if validated, is a **candidate-controlled decision evidence network** connecting a candidate's supported facts, a live vacancy, a verified employer, an effective-dated mobility rule bundle, application claims, corrections and observed outcomes.

That is narrower than the current product surface and harder to execute. It also gives AutoTime a plausible category position that is not simply “European Simplify” or “Jobbatical for consumers.”

## Candidate-side suite evidence

Simplify+ lists autofill, resume tailoring, application writing, networking, outreach and job targeting at $39.99 monthly. Teal+ lists unlimited tracking/resume features and AI tools at $29 monthly. Huntr Pro lists job tracking, tailored resumes, autofill and AI cover letters at $40 monthly. Careerflow advertises a broad career-readiness suite at $23.99 monthly.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

These prices are not direct willingness-to-pay evidence for AutoTime. They show that broad job-search software is bundled around approximately $24–$40 per month and often subsidised by free tiers. AutoTime would struggle to charge a premium for tracking, generic matching, CV writing or autofill alone.

The suites' strategic advantages are installed extensions, accumulated profiles and jobs, established acquisition channels, workflow breadth and high-frequency interaction. Their weakness relative to the proposed AutoTime position is that advertised value centres on speed, materials and organisation rather than governed cross-border feasibility.

However, this weakness is not durable by itself. Each could add country checklists or LLM-generated immigration explanations quickly. A UI feature lead is likely measured in weeks, not years.

## Employer-side mobility evidence

Jobbatical markets pre-hiring immigration assessment, “instant” eligibility checks, visa/work-permit management and expert support in 45+ countries. Localyze markets expert-supported cross-border case management, HRIS integrations, destination services and compliance controls. Deel markets expert immigration services across 75+ countries and exposes an Immigration API.[^5][^6][^7]

The upper competitive bound is stronger still. Fragomen markets technology and legal operations across 170+ jurisdictions and describes a 25+ person knowledge group maintaining 50,000 research data points across 130 countries. Envoy Global markets pre-hire eligibility assessment with legal guidance, data validation, case/document workflow, HRIS connectivity and direct access to legal teams.[^9][^10] These are vendor descriptions, not independent proof of correctness or outcomes. They nevertheless invalidate any moat thesis based only on country breadth, structured rules, citations, expert access, case history or enterprise assurance.

These companies have advantages AutoTime should not attempt to recreate early:

- employer contracts and case volumes;
- expert delivery teams and partner networks;
- direct access to final application facts and outcomes;
- organisational compliance budgets;
- integrations with HRIS/payroll and immigration workflows;
- operational knowledge from exceptions and authority interaction.

AutoTime's possible advantage is temporal and user-aligned: it can help the candidate decide **before** an employer starts a mobility case, including whether a vacancy is worth pursuing and what evidence is missing. That proposition must be tested. It may be difficult to monetise because candidates have uncertain outcomes and employers ultimately control sponsorship.

## Public and general-AI substitution

The EU Talent Pool is intended to let third-country jobseekers register skills and let employers in participating Member States publish vacancies for shortage occupations. Participation is voluntary, and implementation coverage remains relevant uncertainty.[^8] EURES already supplies cross-border jobs and labour information; ESCO provides multilingual occupation/skill taxonomies and APIs.

Public infrastructure can compress AutoTime's discovery and taxonomy advantages. General AI can summarise official pages and generate checklists. AutoTime must therefore regard sources and taxonomies as infrastructure, not proprietary assets.

The proprietary asset must be the governed relationship among:

`candidate fact -> evidence span -> vacancy fact -> employer identity -> effective rule -> decision -> approved claim -> action -> correction/outcome`

The moat can therefore emerge only from **system integration plus accumulated governed evidence**. No single component has strong protection.

## Incumbent advantage and the narrower proprietary loop

AutoTime starts behind enterprise incumbents on legal staffing, jurisdiction count, customer relationships, filings, security assurance and exception history. It starts behind candidate suites on distribution, extensions, job capture and habitual usage. The strategic opportunity is the gap between them, not either flank's established workflow.

The smallest potentially compounding loop is:

`candidate-supported fact -> vacancy-specific evidence -> employer identity -> effective rule -> apply/skip/review decision -> user action -> correction -> verified outcome -> improved evaluation`

The public rules and citations are inputs. The potentially proprietary assets are consented longitudinal candidate evidence, resolved employer/vacancy identities, labelled disagreements, decision-to-action links, and outcomes that can improve calibration. Even those assets become a moat only if they reduce repeated work or measurably improve appropriate decisions.

The moat thesis fails if any of these become true:

- users do not return with multiple vacancies;
- users accept generic AI guidance without provenance;
- employers' willingness to sponsor cannot be verified before application;
- outcomes cannot be collected with acceptable consent and selection-bias controls;
- expert and source operations make each decision uneconomic;
- an incumbent or job platform embeds a sufficiently trusted candidate pre-check at zero marginal price.

This changes the product strategy: integrate or partner for filing and broad workflow; concentrate proprietary engineering on evidence provenance, entity resolution, replay, correction and outcome calibration; and earn each corridor before adding geographic breadth.

## Counter-moat scenarios

### 1. Jobbatical moves candidate-side

Jobbatical already has assessment logic, country coverage and experts. A free candidate pre-check could become an employer lead-generation funnel. This is the most strategically credible adjacent attack.

**Response:** own vacancy-level decisions across employers, maintain candidate portability, and partner rather than compete on filing. Validate whether candidates trust independence from employer incentives.

### 2. Simplify adds visa filters and screens

Simplify has extension distribution and vacancy/profile context. It could generate sponsorship indicators and country explanations rapidly.

**Response:** make every conclusion reproducible and correction-aware; provide authoritative employer identity and exact evidence gaps. Do not attempt to beat Simplify on form coverage.

### 3. Public platforms improve matching

The EU Talent Pool or national portals could connect shortage occupations, third-country profiles and employers at low/no cost.

**Response:** treat them as acquisition/data partners; specialise in decision integrity across markets and application evidence. If public systems deliver trusted personalised feasibility, narrow further or stop.

### 4. LLM answers become “good enough”

General assistants can retrieve and explain official guidance. Many users may accept probabilistic answers instead of paying for governance.

**Response:** test whether provenance, sponsor verification and saved/replayed decisions change behaviour enough to command payment. If not, the moat thesis fails.

### 5. Maintenance burden overwhelms economics

Every market adds source monitoring, translation, tests, experts and incident exposure. Breadth may create negative scale economies before automation matures.

**Response:** country packs earn release independently. Retire or pause markets whose fully loaded support cost exceeds contribution.

## Strategic wedge

The recommended first wedge is:

> For non-EU software, data, product and security professionals evaluating German and Dutch vacancies, AutoTime shows whether the role is actionable, whether the employer is authoritatively verifiable, what evidence controls the conclusion, what remains unknown, and how a rule change would alter the result.

This wedge is valuable only if it produces three observable effects:

1. fewer applications to roles that cannot support the candidate;
2. more completion of evidence needed for viable roles;
3. repeat use on new vacancies with willingness to pay.

“More interviews” remains prohibited as a claim until an appropriate design establishes it.

## Investment gates

Do not fund 20-market production breadth until Germany/Netherlands demonstrate:

- at least 80% user comprehension of result, uncertainty and next action;
- measurable apply/skip changes that reviewers judge appropriate;
- repeated vacancy evaluation by at least half of activated target users;
- real paid transactions rather than stated willingness;
- zero known false “verified sponsor” results;
- 100% replayability for sampled decisions;
- fully loaded contribution-margin path including expert/source operations;
- a written UK boundary opinion before candidate-specific UK outputs.

## Phase 2 correction: the bar is higher than a rules engine

Current evidence shows that employer-side systems already combine several components previously easy to overvalue:

- Jobbatical advertises candidate eligibility checks, case automation, document collection, audit trail and an expert reviewing every filing.
- Localyze advertises a pre-check that can become a case, self-managed or expert-supported execution, document/timeline tracking and HRIS integrations. Its public price list demonstrates a per-case service economy rather than a low-priced candidate subscription economy.
- Deel exposes immigration APIs for requirements, visa types, cases, documents and right-to-work, including a documented recruitment-screening use case.

Therefore `rules + citations + audit log` is not a sufficient moat claim. AutoTime's differentiated asset must satisfy all of these conditions together:

1. candidate-controlled and portable across employers;
2. used before application/case initiation at a specific vacancy;
3. exact employer/vacancy evidence rather than country-only screening;
4. immutable source/rule/decision replay and correction;
5. longitudinal evidence of whether recommendations changed appropriate application behaviour;
6. lower evidence re-entry and review cost as the same candidate evaluates new roles.

If target users do not value that independence and history enough to return or pay, employer-platform APIs will commoditise the assessment layer and AutoTime should partner or narrow further.
