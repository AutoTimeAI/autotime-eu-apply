# Jurisdiction dossiers — Wave 3

**Audit date:** 11 September 2026  
**Scope:** Finland, Belgium, Luxembourg and Poland.

## Finland — Specialist and EU Blue Card

Migri publishes EUR 3,937 monthly for both routes in 2026, but equal amounts do not make the routes interchangeable. The specialist route concerns expert duties and normally a degree or expertise from experience/education. The Blue Card has its own route conditions. Fringe benefits and daily allowances are excluded; the Blue Card amount is reviewed annually.

The statutory Blue Card layer is now anchored in Act 224/2024, section 5: a contract or binding offer of at least six months, pay at least the average gross employee salary, high professional qualifications and any profession-specific qualification, permission or health evidence. It grants the card for two years, or contract duration plus three months where shorter, capped at two years. This 2024 specialist statute supersedes the older Aliens Act formulation that used a one-year contract and a 1.5 multiplier, so the rules engine must not ingest the historical provision as current.

The compiler needs `route`, `cash_base_salary`, excluded components, duties, degree/experience evidence and effective year. Classification as special expertise remains a review point. The remaining source dependency is the reproducible annual calculation trail from the statutory average-salary formula to Migri's EUR 3,937 figure.

## Belgium — three regional systems

The competent authority follows the work region. Flanders, Brussels and Wallonia must be separate bundles.

### Flanders

The implementing rules effective 1 January 2026 require higher qualifications, a highly qualified function and generally 100% of average gross annual salary, with an 80% branch for enumerated local-employment groups. In August 2026 the authority still used EUR 48,912/EUR 39,129.60 while waiting for Statbel's successor statistics.

Only remuneration known with certainty at start counts. Local Belgian contracts may use 13.92 where a thirteenth month and double holiday pay apply; posting uses 12. Some benefits may count, while variable pay, unforeseen bonuses and expenses do not. The current pending-statistic status is an ideal source-change quarantine test.

### Brussels

The Brussels Government Decree of 16 May 2024 article 22 requires an indefinite or at least six-month contract, 100% of Brussels average gross monthly salary, and higher qualifications or a three-in-seven ICT experience branch. From 1 January 2026, monthly thresholds include EUR 3,703.44 for highly qualified staff and EUR 4,748 for Blue Card, with separate ICT manager/expert/trainee values. The work category and region must precede threshold selection. Canonical eJustice retrieval remains a source-quality task because the captured full text is an Official Gazette mirror.

### Wallonia

The Walloon Government Decree of 6 June 2024 anchors the Blue Card branch: indefinite or at least six-month employment, annually adjusted salary, higher qualifications, and a three-relevant-years-in-seven alternative for ICT managers and specialists. Published 2026 annual thresholds include EUR 53,220 highly qualified, EUR 42,576 under 30, EUR 68,815 Blue Card and EUR 55,052 junior Blue Card. Salary-composition and exact annual-index calculation remain to be joined.

## Luxembourg — EU Blue Card

Guichet's March 2026 guidance requires a contract of at least six months, EUR 65,652 annually and high professional qualifications. Before recruitment, the employer declares the vacancy to ADEM. The product therefore needs candidate, offer and employer-process evidence; employer identity does not prove the declaration occurred.

The underlying Grand-Ducal amount regulation and historical supersession chain remain unresolved. Current guidance can power a sourced checklist and provisional match, not a definitive conclusion.

## Poland — EU Blue Card

MOS gives a transparent 2026 derivation: 1.5 × PLN 8,903.56 × 12 = PLN 160,264.08 annually, with a contract of at least six months. The value is keyed to application year because the statistic is the prior year's national-economy average.

From 27 April 2026 temporary-residence filing moved to MOS-only electronic submission; other filing modes have no filing effect. Local occupation refusal lists and regulated-profession checks remain material. The product should expose the process change as dated general information, not personalised legal instruction.
