# Market Priority Decision Model

## Decision update

Germany and the Netherlands remain the strongest paired beachhead, but the evidence changes the expansion order: **Poland moves from watchlist to Wave-2 research**. Its 5,853 EU Blue Cards in 2024 were second only to Germany, and its official MOS system now exposes a 2026 salary threshold and digital application workflow.[^1][^2]

This is precisely why a scored narrative written before route-volume and current implementation evidence is unsafe.

The broader evidence also constrains market-size storytelling. EU issuance fell from 89,055 in 2023 to 78,096 in 2024, while five countries represented 89.5% of 2024 volume. Eurostat's statistical unit can count the same person more than once across different procedures or multiple cards. Blue Card issuance is therefore a useful revealed-activity signal, not unique-customer TAM, and the 2024 fall cannot be interpreted as proven demand contraction.

## Model design

The model separates evidence from judgement and applies hard gates after scoring.

| Dimension                           | Weight | Evidence sought                                             | Why it matters                           |
| ----------------------------------- | -----: | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Revealed skilled-mobility volume    |    20% | Blue Cards/permits issued and trend                         | Stronger than nominal route availability |
| Target-tech labour context          |    15% | ICT employment, shortages/vacancies and salary fit          | Approximates corridor opportunity        |
| Employer/action verifiability       |    15% | Sponsor/certified employer data and legal identifiers       | Enables differentiated vacancy decisions |
| Rule determinism and source quality |    15% | Current amounts, lists, workflows and primary-law chain     | Controls automation feasibility          |
| Candidate wedge fit                 |    15% | Frequency of pre-application uncertainty and evidence reuse | Controls customer value and retention    |
| Reachability and willingness to pay |    10% | Search/community access, activation and paid tests          | Controls commercial viability            |
| Operational/regulatory cost         |    10% | Languages, regions, advice boundary, expert/source cost     | Controls contribution margin             |

Scores are unusable if material cells lack evidence. Unknowns receive a conservative prior and a confidence penalty; they are not silently treated as average.

## Hard gates

Regardless of weighted score, a market cannot reach production support without:

- complete decisive official source chain;
- no unresolved critical contradiction;
- current threshold/list data and monitored dependencies;
- deterministic and adversarial corpus passing;
- expert sign-off and approved output policy;
- replay, correction and incident readiness;
- a credible market-specific contribution path.

## Evidence-supported bands

This ordering is deliberately banded rather than given false decimal precision.

### Band A — production-track hypothesis

**Germany**

- 56,252 Blue Cards in 2024, 72% of EU issuance.[^1]
- Large absolute ICT workforce: approximately 2.3 million in 2025.[^3]
- Current official salary and ICT-experience rules are structured.
- Weakness: no simple national sponsor register; qualification/job matching and BA dependencies add complexity.

**Netherlands**

- Authoritative recognised-sponsor register updated monthly.
- Current age/context salary thresholds are explicit.
- Q2 2025 economy-wide vacancy rate of 4.2% was the EU's highest, although this is not tech-specific and must not be overgeneralised.[^4]
- CBS records 16,085 non-EU/EFTA knowledge-migrant arrivals in 2024 and 13,905 in 2025, down 46.9% from 26,180 in 2022.[^6] These are migration-motive arrivals, not permit decisions, active applicants or unique addressable customers.
- Weakness: the sharp decline removes any credible Netherlands growth narrative, while technology occupation share and sponsor hiring activity remain unknown.

The pair is strategically useful because it tests two different moat primitives: rule-depth at scale and employer verification.

### Band B — accelerated research

**Poland**

- Second-highest 2024 Blue Card issuance (5,853).[^1]
- 2026 official MOS guidance states annual gross remuneration above PLN 160,264.08 and a six-month contract minimum.[^2]
- Digital-only MOS submissions from 27 April 2026 create a current workflow surface.[^5]
- Weakness: local surplus-occupation lists and implementation changes require careful governance.

**Ireland**

- Tech occupations are explicitly represented in the Critical Skills system.
- Official occupation list effective 13 May 2026 and current processing-date transparency.
- Weakness: official pages disagree by small amounts on 2026 thresholds; controlling-instrument resolution is mandatory.

**France and Spain**

- Third and fourth among the selected EU markets by revealed 2024 Blue Card issuance (2,775 and 1,924).[^1]
- Large tech labour contexts, particularly France and Spain.[^3]
- Weakness: parallel routes, language/source maintenance and salary implementation need stronger chains.

Band B order should be tested under origin-market demand and paid acquisition evidence; it is not a launch sequence yet.

### Band C — structured specialist opportunities

**Sweden, Austria, Denmark and Finland** have strong tech shares and structured rules, but smaller revealed Blue Card volumes or parallel national schemes. Denmark and Ireland do not participate in the EU Blue Card system, so national permit data—not Blue Card absence—must drive comparison. Austria's labour-market test and multiple routes add complexity. Finland's specialist route is relatively machine-readable.

**Luxembourg** is deterministic and has a high ICT employment share, but its market scale may make it better as a premium corridor than an acquisition beachhead.

### Band D — source/operations-first

**Belgium** requires regional rule packs plus federal residence dependencies. **Portugal** has attractive highly qualified and Tech Visa concepts but current administrative/source-age risks. **Italy** still requires collective-agreement and current-statistic mapping. **Czechia and Lithuania** now have current amount/effective-date chains, but still need vacancy/list ingestion and market-demand evidence. **Estonia** has low revealed Blue Card volume and a separate top-specialist route. These remain evidence-gated investments, not production promises.

### Separate operating models

**United Kingdom** has strong market relevance and a sponsor register but a material regulated-advice boundary. **Switzerland** is quota-, employer- and canton-dependent. **Norway** uses a national skilled-worker model with contextual qualification and normal-conditions tests. Each should be assessed as a separate product/partner case, not ranked as another Blue Card country.

## Sensitivity tests required

The final ranking must be recomputed under at least four strategies:

1. **Revenue-first:** increase reachability/WTP and market scale weights.
2. **Moat-first:** increase employer verifiability, rule complexity and reusable evidence.
3. **Low-capital:** increase inverse operations cost and deterministic source quality.
4. **Partner-led:** increase employer/adviser ecosystem and reduce penalty for regulated fulfilment.

If Germany/Netherlands loses under most plausible weights, revisit the beachhead. If Poland remains high across strategies, promote it to the next full country dossier.

## Missing data that can change the result

- National skilled/highly-qualified permit volumes where Blue Card is not the dominant route.
- Country-by-country ICT vacancy and salary distributions for the target occupations.
- Origin-country search demand and candidate flow into each destination.
- Number and hiring activity of verified/certified sponsors.
- English-language role share and remote/application accessibility.
- Paid conversion and repeat-use results by corridor.
- Fully loaded expert/source/support cost per market.
- Refusal, withdrawal and processing-time distributions where available.
