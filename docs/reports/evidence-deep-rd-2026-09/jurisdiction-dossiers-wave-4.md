# Jurisdiction dossiers — Wave 4

**Audit date:** 11 September 2026  
**Scope:** Norway, Switzerland, United Kingdom, Italy, Portugal, Estonia, Lithuania and Czechia.

## Norway — Skilled worker

UDI requires a concrete offer from one specific employer and appropriate skilled qualifications. In no-collective-agreement cases, its published benchmarks are NOK 624,700 for master-required and NOK 545,400 for bachelor-required positions; a lower figure requires substantial evidence that the pay is normal. Benefits/expense coverage must not be silently folded into salary.

Since 19 February 2026, relevant candidates self-filing from abroad require an employer confirmation code. This is strong vacancy authenticity evidence, but only for its stated workflow. Qualification equivalence, position level, collective agreement and normal-pay exceptions remain review points.

## Switzerland — third-country employment

The source chain exposes an inherently discretionary system: overall economic interest, annual quotas, Swiss/EU-EFTA precedence, customary regional/sector pay and conditions, and highly qualified candidate criteria. The employer applies and must evidence recruitment efforts. Canton and live quota are material.

The correct product is an evidence checklist and employer/cantonal-review state. It is not a deterministic visa score. No `eligible` output is appropriate from candidate/vacancy facts alone.

## United Kingdom — Skilled Worker

Appendix Skilled Worker is a multi-branch points system. Mandatory requirements include valid sponsorship, skill level and English. Salary options include standard, relevant PhD, STEM PhD, Immigration Salary List, new entrant, health/education and transitional cohorts, each paired with occupation going rates. A Certificate of Sponsorship is specific, time-limited and does more than sponsor-register presence; the genuine-job test remains separate.

The live sponsor register is mutable. Occupation and salary lists have removal dates. A 3 September 2026 Statement of Changes postdates the Appendix update date shown as 3 August, so announcement, commencement and incorporated text must be tracked separately. Pending written IAA scope advice, outputs remain sourced general information and fact verification—not personalised eligibility advice.

## Italy — EU Blue Card

The national Article 27-quater chain supersedes a simplistic single-number model. Remuneration must satisfy the relevant nationally representative collective agreement and cannot fall below ISTAT annual average gross remuneration. Candidate routes include tertiary qualification, five years relevant experience, or ICT manager/specialist three-in-seven experience. The offer lasts at least six months and the employer files through the One-Stop-Shop.

The government integration portal currently describes the ISTAT floor as approximately EUR 36,300, but that is not an exact, dated statistical observation suitable for boundary decisions. Labour Ministry material confirms that Italy has no single statutory wage floor and collective agreements remain central by employer economic category. The compiler therefore needs the exact ISTAT observation plus employer sector, applicable CCNL identifier, classification level and current pay table. Until those resolve, salary evaluation is `insufficient_evidence` even where offered salary exceeds EUR 36,300.

## Portugal — EU Blue Card and Tech Visa

AIMA confirms a six-month highly qualified offer and annual salary of 1.5 times national average gross annual salary, or 1.2 times in specified cases. Tech Visa is separate: certified employer, 12-month contract, qualification/language and IAS-derived pay conditions. AIMA's electronic Blue Card platform is described as in implementation.

The 2026 Tech Visa IAS input is now resolved by Portaria 480-A/2025: EUR 537.13 from 1 January 2026, expressly revoking the 2025 predecessor. At 2.5 times IAS the mathematical result is EUR 1,342.825; the product must not silently choose a cent-rounding rule without route-specific confirmation. The Blue Card's current national-average statistic and reduced-case mapping remain dependencies.

The Tech Visa employer chain is stronger than previously recorded. IAPMEI publishes corporate tax number, legal name, certification date and expiry, and offers candidate-specific access-code validation for the mandatory responsibility term. AutoTime can therefore verify exact legal identity and date-valid certification, but must keep company certification distinct from the candidate's responsibility term. A brand-name match or expired list row is not verified sponsorship.

## Estonia — EU Blue Card and top specialist

These routes must not be conflated. The top-specialist permit generally uses 1.5 times the latest annual average gross monthly wage and adds company registration/age/economic conditions. The Blue Card separately uses 1.5 times, with 1.24 times for enumerated professional groups.

Statistics Estonia reports 2025 annual average monthly gross wages of EUR 2,092, mathematically producing EUR 3,138.00 and EUR 2,594.08. Administrative confirmation of the intended series and rounding is still required. Route selection and occupation mapping come first.

## Lithuania — EU Blue Card

The law supports a six-month offer and 1.2 times latest average wage for listed high-value shortage occupations or 1.5 times otherwise. The 2026 successor is now resolved: the Migration Department published BDU of EUR 2,411.40, with EUR 2,893.68 at 1.2 and EUR 3,617.10 at 1.5, for applications accepted from 12 May 2026.

The transition is not a simple overwrite. Intermediary letters completed and paid before 12 May remain receivable but employers are asked to confirm the new pay; letters still using the old BDU after 25 May are returned for correction. The rules engine therefore needs application-acceptance date, intermediary-letter state, branch/list version and salary—not merely a current threshold. The current high-value shortage list version remains the principal open source.

## Czechia — EU Blue Card

The Ministry portal requires high qualification, a qualifying job, a contract/pre-contract and 1.5 times average gross salary. Current Aliens Act section 42j resolves the live duration rule at six months; older ministry material that states one year is stale and must not control current decisions. The application uses a vacancy identifier from the central Blue Card vacancy evidence; labour-law publication logic can include a 30-day condition.

MPSV announcement 44/2026 sets the reference average gross annual wage at CZK 590,580 from 1 May 2026 through 30 April 2027. The statutory 1.5 multiplier therefore yields CZK 885,870 annually or CZK 73,822.50 monthly. The reference statistic, derived threshold and effective interval must remain separate fields. Vacancy presence is evidence, not eligibility.
