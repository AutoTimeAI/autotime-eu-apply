# Commercial Validation Model

## Commercial question

The key question is not whether international technologists experience uncertainty. It is whether AutoTime can repeatedly acquire a narrowly defined group, change valuable application decisions, and charge enough to support rule, expert and evidence operations.

## Initial customer and job

**Beachhead persona:** non-EU software, data, security or product professional applying from outside Europe to German and Dutch roles, with 3–10 years' experience, an English CV, uncertain sponsorship feasibility and multiple vacancies to evaluate.

**Job to be done:** before spending time tailoring and applying, determine whether the role and employer are actionable, why, what is missing, and which evidence should be prepared.

Excluded initially: asylum/family routes; regulated professions; self-employment/founder routes; candidates already represented by counsel; broad relocation logistics; and definitive UK personalised route advice.

## Bottom-up addressability equation

Do not publish a single TAM until the inputs are sourced. Use:

```text
annual_addressable_candidates
  = target-origin candidates actively seeking DE/NL tech work
  × share meeting experience/role scope
  × share encountering employer-dependent mobility uncertainty

annual_serviceable_decisions
  = annual_addressable_candidates
  × relevant vacancies evaluated per candidate
  × reachable acquisition share

annual_revenue
  = activated candidates
  × paid conversion
  × average paid months or packs
  × realised net price
```

Every variable requires low/base/high values, source or experiment owner, retrieval date and confidence. EU-wide ICT employment is context, not a valid numerator for candidate demand.

## Offer experiments

Test real payment before building broad market coverage:

| Offer                        |   Test price | Included                                                           | Hypothesis                                           |
| ---------------------------- | -----------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Vacancy Decision Pack        | £19 one-time | Five DE/NL vacancy decisions with evidence gaps and sponsor checks | Candidates pay to avoid wasted applications          |
| Evidence Workspace           |    £29/month | Ongoing decisions, saved evidence and change alerts                | Repeat vacancy evaluation creates subscription value |
| Human-Reviewed Corridor Pack | £99 one-time | Bounded software assessment plus qualified review where permitted  | Trust and ambiguity support premium economics        |

Prices are experiments informed by candidate-suite anchors, not validated pricing. Randomise or sequence offers carefully; capture refusals and alternative spend.

## Concierge UAT protocol

Minimum first round: five target users, ten real vacancies each.

For every vacancy capture:

1. unaided apply/skip/unsure decision and confidence;
2. time spent and sources consulted;
3. AutoTime result, reasons, evidence gaps and uncertainty;
4. user comprehension in their own words;
5. post-result decision and reason for change;
6. correction/disagreement and supporting evidence;
7. application action and later outcome where consented;
8. payment decision using a real checkout.

Do not coach users during the comprehension question. A correct answer must identify the route/status, decisive fact, uncertainty and next action.

## Success and falsification gates

Continue after the first 50 vacancy decisions only if:

- at least 80% of result explanations are correctly understood;
- at least 20% of decisions change, and expert review judges at least 80% of changes appropriate;
- at least 3 of 5 users complete a second session with new vacancies;
- at least 3 users buy an offered pack or subscription without reimbursement;
- no false sponsor verification occurs;
- users identify decision confidence/evidence as the value, not merely CV writing;
- median support/review time shows a plausible gross-margin path.

Pause or change the thesis if users cannot distinguish conditional from eligible; primarily request generic documents/autofill; refuse to pay after receiving value; or expert review frequently overturns apparently deterministic results.

## Unit-economics ledger

```text
net revenue
- payment fees and taxes borne by AutoTime
- model/extraction/embedding cost
- source retrieval and storage
- expert review and country-operations labour
- customer support
- correction, refund and dispute cost
- allocated security/compliance cost
= contribution
```

Track per user, decision, country and route. Expert review cannot be hidden as founder labour. Country gross margin must include monitoring and annual re-review even when no paid user triggers a case.

## Acquisition experiments

Prioritise high-intent channels that expose the decision problem:

- SEO pages around exact employer/route questions, with current sources and honest unknowns;
- partnerships with international tech communities and alumni groups;
- candidate referrals after a useful saved decision;
- relocation-job boards where permitted;
- regulated adviser referral loops for complex/ready cases;
- employer or recruiter pilots only after candidate-side independence is understood.

Measure qualified activation and paid conversion, not impressions. Avoid paid acquisition until the concierge flow demonstrates repeat value; otherwise CAC optimises traffic into an unvalidated proposition.

## Outcome evidence limitations

Application outcomes are confounded by seniority, skill, language, labour market, employer demand and candidate selection. AutoTime must not infer causal interview improvement from users who choose stronger roles. Early evidence should focus on nearer causal mechanisms: unsuitable applications avoided, missing evidence resolved, unsupported claims blocked, sponsor status correctly understood and time to confident decision.

“More interviews” requires a later comparison design with pre-specified cohorts, consistent outcome definitions, sufficient follow-up and missing-outcome analysis.

## Commercial moat evidence

Commercial defensibility is earned when:

- candidates accumulate reusable, confirmed evidence and return;
- saved decisions become more valuable through source changes/replay;
- corrections improve rule/entity mapping for future bounded cases;
- expert review cost per decision falls without lower agreement;
- trusted referral/partner channels lower acquisition cost;
- the product's evidence loop—not generic features—predicts retention.

Until those observations exist, the moat remains an engineering and market hypothesis.
