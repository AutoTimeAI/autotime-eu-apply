## Findings that changed the model

1. **Italy:** national law does not support a naive single salary threshold. It requires the applicable collective-agreement remuneration and no less than ISTAT's annual gross average. Product consequence: two independently versioned inputs and manual review where agreement mapping is uncertain.
2. **Estonia:** EU Blue Card remuneration is not the same rule as the top-specialist permit. The Blue Card contains 1.5× and enumerated 1.24× branches. Product consequence: route selection precedes salary comparison.
3. **Lithuania:** the Migration Department's May 2025 figures include an explicit application effective date. Product consequence: amount versions must bind to application/proceeding time, but these figures cannot be assumed current in September 2026.
4. **Portugal:** current AIMA guidance confirms the 1.5×/1.2× formula and six-month duration, but the referenced statistic and reduced-case mapping remain dependencies.
5. **Czechia:** statutory vacancy publication logic strengthens the vacancy identifier as evidence but does not make presence equivalent to candidate eligibility.
6. **Estonia numeric chain:** Statistics Estonia's latest published annual series reports EUR 2,092 for 2025. The statutory multipliers mathematically produce EUR 3,138.00 and EUR 2,594.08, but administrative confirmation and rounding treatment remain required before production activation.
7. **Germany:** §18g confirms the annual-notice architecture, six-month offer and three-in-seven IT experience branch with degree-level comparability and job-necessity predicates.
8. **Netherlands:** threshold selection depends on application or new-employment start date; compensation composition is rule-critical, and the live sponsor register had a 3 September 2026 version.
9. **Ireland:** the Act requires annual remuneration review, while the 2026 amendment must be overlaid on the 2024 regulation. The existing official-page contradiction remains fail-closed.
10. **Sweden:** the current amount is explicitly effective 15 July 2026 and derived from 1.25 times a National Mediation Office statistic, creating a clean formula/statistic/effective-date chain once national law is added.
11. **France:** the 2025 order creates an explicit supersession edge to the 2016 orders and a reproducible EUR 59,373 threshold.
12. **Spain:** threshold changes become applicable one month after a new INE survey and old applications form a separate cohort; this demands bitemporal application logic.
13. **Austria:** EUR 55,678 includes special-payment semantics and the AMS labour-market test is an authority-side unknown, not a candidate eligibility input.
14. **Denmark:** first applications and extensions can use different threshold versions; Pay Limit and Supplementary Pay Limit are separate route bundles.
15. **Finland:** specialist and Blue Card share a 2026 number but not a rule bundle; fringe benefits and daily allowances are excluded.
16. **Belgium:** region, local/posted arrangement, compensation components and divisor all affect evaluation; Flanders has an announced pending source dependency.
17. **Luxembourg:** candidate and offer facts must be joined with employer ADEM vacancy-declaration evidence; identity alone is insufficient.
18. **Poland:** the amount is keyed to application year and the filing workflow changed to MOS-only on 27 April 2026.
19. **Norway:** employer confirmation is an anti-fraud primitive only for specified self-filed-abroad workflows; salary exceptions require normal-pay evidence.
20. **Switzerland:** quota, precedence, canton and customary conditions make expert/authority review intrinsic rather than a temporary data gap.
21. **United Kingdom:** the route is multi-branch, occupation- and cohort-dependent; sponsor register presence is insufficient, and announced-versus-commenced changes need separate states.
22. **Competitor correction:** Jobbatical, Localyze and Deel already combine screening, cases, documents, audit/compliance and experts; Deel also distributes assessment through APIs. A rules engine with citations is not itself a moat.
23. **Pricing structure:** candidate suites cluster around roughly USD 24-40 monthly, while published mobility services are often hundreds or thousands per case. AutoTime must test whether candidate pre-application value supports subscription, per-decision or employer/partner economics.
24. **Economics correction:** the £19 five-decision pack has only £3.80 gross revenue per decision before fees/tax, so routine human review cannot fit. Review must be exception-based or separately priced.
25. **Market ranking discipline:** absent national-route volumes are unknown, not zero; Blue Card non-participants require separate denominators. The new evidence matrix prevents false cross-route ranking precision.
26. **AI Act boundary:** employer recruitment/selection is expressly Annex III high-risk. Candidate-side self-assessment is not a proven exemption; intended purpose and deployment must be controlled and reviewed.
27. **UK privacy freshness:** ICO automated-decision guidance is under revision after the Data (Use and Access) Act, creating a monitored winter-2026 dependency.
28. **Immutability correction:** audit integrity cannot mean indefinite undeletable personal data; immutable event/version metadata must be separable from erasable or anonymisable candidate payloads.
29. **France contradiction:** the public France-Visas duration and current law-level duration do not align. The disputed 6–12 month band is now an explicit `source_conflict` test.
