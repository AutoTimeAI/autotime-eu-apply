# AutoTime EU Apply: Startup Moat R&D Dossier

**Decision date:** 10 September 2026  
**Foundation:** AutoTime Core Product Investment Strategy  
**Purpose:** determine where sustained product, research, data and operations investment can create a defensible company.

## Founder research principle

> **Deepness provides clarity, and clarity makes me clever.**

This is an operating principle for AutoTime R&D. Research should go deep enough to improve founder
judgment and expose assumptions, dependencies, contradictions, economics, technical consequences
and failure modes. Speed or document length is not the success measure. The success measure is
whether the evidence creates clearer business and product decisions.

Future R&D may take hours or longer when reliability requires it. A deliverable is not complete
because its headings are filled: material findings must be sourced or labelled as assumptions,
unresolved questions must have owners and safe interim behavior, and recommendations must have
acceptance or falsification criteria.

AutoTime research must serve both sides of the company:

- **Startup business:** market, customer, differentiation, distribution, pricing, economics,
  regulation, defensibility, organization, risks and investment decisions.
- **IT product:** architecture, data, security, privacy, AI boundaries, evaluation, operations,
  maintenance, migration, reliability and release governance.

## Founder conclusion

AutoTime should become a **cross-border application decision and evidence system**, not a broad AI
job-search suite. Its compounding loop is:

`candidate evidence -> governed role decision -> supported claims -> human approval -> outcome learning`

The moat is not an LLM, score, prompt, extension or country database. It can emerge from five
assets used together: a versioned candidate evidence graph; governed bitemporal mobility policy; a
reproducible decision compiler; a claim-to-evidence application compiler; and consent-compatible
correction, reliance and outcome data.

The most important R&D finding is also a warning. Personalized mobility recommendations are the
highest-value behavior and the sharpest regulatory boundary. UK Immigration Advice Authority
guidance distinguishes general information from an opinion on actions based on an individual's
circumstances.[^1] Product scope, adviser involvement and jurisdiction routing must be architecture,
not disclaimer text.

Until repeat paid use and measured decision improvement exist, this remains a moat hypothesis.

### Five founder decisions

1. Adopt **cross-border application decision and evidence system** as the company thesis.
2. Validate Germany/Netherlands as the first paired beachhead; do not market four equal corridors.
3. Freeze personalized UK mobility conclusions until written regulatory scope is obtained.
4. Put the evidence/decision/claim spine ahead of new workflow breadth for two quarters.
5. Require paid repeat use, appropriate reliance and fully loaded margin before calling this a moat.

## Competitive boundary

Simplify leads with broad autofill; Teal, Huntr, Careerflow and Jobscan bundle resume tailoring,
matching, tracking and application workflow.[^2][^3][^4] Teal currently lists USD 29 per 30 days
and Huntr USD 40 monthly.[^5][^6] Competing on documents, keywords, application count or supported
sites puts AutoTime in a mature commodity category.

EURES already provides cross-border jobs and information; ESCO provides versioned multilingual
occupation and skill identifiers and APIs.[^7][^8] AutoTime should use these as infrastructure. Its
proprietary question is:

> Given this candidate's supported facts, this vacancy, this country and current governed rules,
> is the role worth pursuing, what remains unknown, and what can be truthfully submitted next?

Position it as cross-border application decision support. Lead with the decision, prove it through
official sources and candidate evidence, and follow through with supported content. Do not lead
with AI, percentages, resume generation, autofill or Europe-wide coverage.

## Beachhead thesis

Public evidence establishes a problem, not AutoTime's revenue. Eurostat reports about 78,100 EU
Blue Cards in 2024; Germany issued about 56,300, or 72 percent. India was the largest recipient
nationality with 16,268.[^9][^10] The EU employed 10.45 million ICT specialists in 2025, while
non-EU citizens experience substantially higher over-qualification.[^11][^12]

The first customer should be an experienced non-EEA Tech/FinTech candidate who is actively applying
in two markets, has credible evidence, faces a sponsorship/permit/salary/employer/qualification
unknown, and values fewer wasted applications over mass submission.

The first discovery hypothesis is **India-origin technology candidates targeting Germany and the
Netherlands**. Germany has observed Blue Card volume. The Netherlands exposes a public recognised-
sponsor register and explicit salary criteria, so a meaningful part of viability is verifiable.[^13]
Retain UK and Ireland as research corridors pending operational and regulatory proof. This is a
test, not a final segment decision.

## Four countries are four governed products

| Corridor    | Verifiable anchors                                             | Main ambiguity                                    | R&D posture                                    |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Netherlands | Recognised sponsor, age/status salary threshold, contract      | Route exceptions, salary composition, market rate | Best verification lab; monthly sponsor refresh |
| Germany     | Salary, qualification/job relation, shortage/new-entrant rules | Recognition and alternative routes                | Begin with narrow Blue Card scenarios          |
| Ireland     | Occupation list, remuneration, qualification, offer duration   | Job-to-SOC classification                         | Require confidence and abstention              |
| UK          | Sponsor, occupation code, going rate, threshold                | Exceptions, frequent changes, advice regulation   | Research until written scope review            |

Official sources show why rule values cannot live in prompts or UI copy. The Dutch highly skilled
migrant route combines recognised sponsor, contract, income and market-rate conditions; its public
sponsor register is updated monthly.[^13][^14] Germany publishes route-specific 2026 Blue Card
salary conditions.[^15] Ireland combines occupation, remuneration, qualification, employer and
offer-duration tests, with thresholds changed in 2026.[^16][^17] The UK combines approved sponsor,
eligible occupation and the higher of the general threshold or going rate, with exceptions.[^18]

Every marketed corridor needs a machine-readable coverage contract:

```yaml
country: NL
pathway: highly_skilled_migrant
supported_scenarios: [standard_under_30, standard_30_plus]
unsupported_scenarios: [cross_border_service_provider]
source_version: nl-hsm-2026.2
valid_from: 2026-07-01
reviewed_at: 2026-09-10
next_review_at: 2026-10-01
reviewer_scope: general_information_only
decision_ceiling: investigate
```

The decision ceiling prevents the system from turning facts into a personalized legal conclusion
when reviewer/regulatory scope permits only general information.

## Regulatory architecture

The UK IAA says general information not related to an individual case is outside its regulated
boundary, while an opinion on a course of action based on supplied personal circumstances can be
immigration advice. Unregulated provision can be a criminal offence.[^1] Before selling personalized
UK mobility decisions, obtain a written opinion from qualified UK counsel, seek IAA clarification
if advised, define professional-review triggers, and encode those limits in policy. Run equivalent
reviews for every marketed country.

Until then, UK output should show official rules, source-backed fact checks, missing facts,
verification routes and regulated-adviser referral. If immigration status is decisive, cap the
recommendation at `Investigate`.

The EU AI Act treats employer-side recruitment and selection systems as high-risk.[^19] AutoTime is
candidate-side and does not make an employer's decision, so classification needs counsel. Any move
into employer screening/ranking requires fresh review.

GDPR Article 22 addresses solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects.[^20]
AutoTime does not grant a job or visa, but profiling, transparency, lawful basis, minimisation,
rights and security still apply. EDPB guidance covers profiling and automated decisions.[^21] Make
a DPIA a launch gate. CNIL guidance treats research and production as separate processing purposes
and requires purpose, legal basis, minimisation, retention and rights.[^22] Never silently use beta
CVs, corrections or outcomes to train models. Intentionally inferred special-category information
remains special-category data under ICO guidance.[^23]

| Data tier           | Default use          | Additional gate                               |
| ------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Raw service data    | Requested workflow   | Notice, basis, minimum retention              |
| Operations metadata | Reliability/security | Strict allowlist; no content                  |
| Product analytics   | Funnel/usability     | Purpose-specific consent/config and retention |
| Evaluation cases    | Locked tests         | De-identification, provenance, review         |
| Model training      | Off                  | Separate basis/consent, withdrawal, necessity |

## Defensibility spine

The repository has useful ingredients: consent metadata, RLS, operational events, AI cost records,
EU Fit helpers, evidence records and an application-kit use case. But the mobility profile remains
a single JSON document and events represent broad stages. That supports beta workflow, not temporal
truth, claim provenance or calibration.

Build an additive domain rather than rewriting the dashboard:

```text
role_case
  -> evidence_fact -> evidence_source -> source_span
  -> vacancy_fact -> vacancy_source
  -> decision_snapshot -> decision_reason -> policy_rule_version
  -> application_artifact -> material_claim -> claim_support
  -> correction / override / review_event -> outcome_event

mobility_source -> source_revision -> policy_rule_version -> governed_scenario
                                              -> expert_signoff
```

Invariants:

- facts are superseded, never destructively rewritten;
- decisions reference exact fact, policy and source versions;
- replay reproduces the historical normalized result;
- each material claim has support or `awaiting_confirmation`;
- generation cannot promote evidence status;
- `Skip` cannot produce a kit without recorded override;
- expired sources lower the maximum decision confidence;
- current-policy comparison never overwrites history;
- analytics rejects free text and unknown properties;
- deletion cascades personal content unless data is demonstrably anonymous.

Mobility knowledge needs two clocks: **valid time**, when a rule legally applied, and **system
time**, when AutoTime learned/reviewed/published it. Store `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `recorded_at`,
`superseded_at`, source revision, review state and typed rule parameters. One `updated_at` cannot
answer "what applied then?" and "what did AutoTime know then?"

Use deterministic code for gates/calculations and LLMs only for typed extraction, normalization and
drafting:

```text
untrusted vacancy/CV -> typed facts + cited spans -> user correction
official revision -> reviewed typed policy
facts + policy -> deterministic decision
decision + evidence -> constrained draft -> claim check -> human approval
```

Never ask a model to remember current thresholds. Vacancy text is untrusted and cannot alter policy
or evidence status.

## Proprietary learning loop

```text
scenario -> decision -> comprehension/correction/override -> reviewed action
 -> outcome -> error analysis -> policy/UX change -> locked regression case
```

Potential assets are: rule revisions mapped to executable conditions; expert-labelled boundary and
counterfactual cases; structured corrections; appropriate-reliance observations; and privacy-minimal
decision-to-outcome links.

Classify corrections as `extraction_error`, `stale_source`, `wrong_rule`, `wrong_country`,
`ambiguous_vacancy`, `candidate_fact_changed`, `explanation_failure` or `user_preference`. Only some
are system defects. Optimizing for user agreement would destroy truthfulness.

A data advantage exists only if later users measurably benefit, improvement survives held-out/time-
split tests, labels are difficult to buy or synthesize, identifiers are unnecessary, and review cost
is below value. If data changes no product decision within a quarter, stop collecting it or document
why retention remains necessary.

## Evaluation science

| Consequence-specific error                         | Proposed release threshold            |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| False green: `Apply` despite verified hard blocker | Zero in critical locked corpus        |
| Certainty despite decisive unknown/stale source    | Zero                                  |
| Unsupported material claim                         | Zero confirmed cases                  |
| Cross-surface drift                                | Zero unexplained cases                |
| False red                                          | Under 2% in locked corpus; review all |
| Wrong recourse                                     | Under 5%; expert review               |

These are internal starting thresholds, not external benchmarks.

Run an appropriate-reliance study with 12-20 target users, 8-12 counterbalanced scenarios each,
correct and deliberately flawed recommendations, and competing explanation formats. Measure unaided
restatement, acceptance, error detection, confidence, time and correction. Report
`P(accept | correct)` and `P(reject | incorrect)` separately. Explanations fail if they increase
acceptance of wrong advice. Research shows explanations do not automatically improve human-AI
decisions or verifiability.[^24][^25]

Use two independent experts per critical case, preserve disagreement, adjudicate critical cases,
record scope/conflicts/date, measure inter-rater agreement, and blind the final challenge set from
policy implementers.

These are test brackets, not approved prices. Run real Stripe checkout; survey intent is secondary.
Generic tools anchor around USD 29-40 monthly, but AutoTime must first prove differentiated trust.[^5][^6]

Calculate per corridor:

`contribution = net revenue - payment fees - AI - storage/email - support - source governance - expert review - refunds`

Founder research time is not free. Starting hypotheses: AI/infrastructure below 10% of net revenue;
support plus variable expert cost below 30%; contribution above 60% before fixed R&D; refunds below
8%; and 30% of activated payers checking a second role within 30 days. Replace these thresholds
after 20 and 50 completed payments.

Payment economics must use the company's actual merchant country, card mix, tax treatment and Stripe
products. For reference, Stripe's UK standard page currently lists 1.5% + GBP 0.20 for standard UK
cards, 2.5% + GBP 0.20 for EEA cards, and Billing pay-as-you-go at 0.7% of billing volume.[^34]
International cards, FX, disputes, tax tooling and refunds can cost more. Store actual fee, tax,
refund and net-settlement values per charge; never estimate realized margin from list prices.

Distribution should concentrate pain: diaspora tech communities by corridor, international alumni,
regulated advisers, relocation specialists, and precise content about sponsor/rule verification.
Avoid broad "AI resume builder" traffic.

Test an adviser handoff packet of candidate-approved facts, unresolved questions, source versions
and history. If it reduces adviser preparation time and yields consented structured corrections, it
can become both a distribution channel and a label-quality advantage.

## Moat falsification

Narrow or abandon the thesis after two focused cohorts if users mainly want commodity automation;
official self-service pages solve the decision; personalized guidance is unlawful or uneconomic;
experts cannot agree; users do not correct/return/report outcomes; source cost outgrows corridor
revenue; decisions cannot beat a simple official checklist; traceability adds time without reducing
errors; or demonstrated willingness to pay is below safe delivery cost.

If consumer decisions fail but adviser packets save professionals material time, consider B2B only
after a fresh AI Act, controller-role and strategy review. Do not drift into employer ranking.

## Adjacent moat feature: vacancy integrity triage

Cross-border candidates face not only eligibility uncertainty but fake jobs, fake sponsorship and
fee scams. UK guidance warns about non-existent jobs, guaranteed visas, fake documents and requests
for fees; Europol notes that fake overseas adverts can enable fraud, identity theft and labour
exploitation.[^32][^33] This is strategically aligned with EU Fit because it changes whether a role
deserves effort and whether candidate evidence should be disclosed.

Add a narrow `Verify employer/offer` state, not a generic fraud score. Checks may include:

- exact official sponsor-register entity and route, with date/source;
- consistency between vacancy brand, employing entity, email domain and stated location;
- requests for candidate-paid sponsorship/recruitment fees;
- claims of guaranteed job or visa;
- offer without an application/interview;
- missing or contradictory employer contact and vacancy details;
- link/domain mismatch and pressure to move to private messaging;
- a direct official-employer verification path.

Never label an employer fraudulent from heuristics. Output `verified official attribute`,
`unverified`, `inconsistent`, or `high-risk pattern - stop and verify`; show the evidence and route
the user to official reporting. Keep uploaded identity and offer documents out of analytics. This
feature should ship only if user research shows it influences decisions without generating damaging
false accusations.

### Minimum team and decision rights

One founder may hold multiple roles, but the decisions must remain distinct:

- product/research lead owns segment hypotheses, protocols and kill decisions;
- decision engineer owns typed policy, snapshots, replay and critical evaluation;
- evidence engineer owns fact/claim provenance and generated-content integrity;
- mobility operations owner owns sources, SLAs, changes and incidents;
- authorised jurisdiction reviewer owns only the legal scope they are qualified for;
- privacy/security owner owns DPIA, lawful-basis map, retention, access and recovery;
- commercial owner owns price tests, refunds and fully loaded economics.

No person who implements a critical rule should be its only approver. A founder can coordinate the
system, but cannot self-declare jurisdiction competence or independent evaluation.

### IP posture

Treat patents as an option, not the moat plan. EPO guidance requires the ordinary novelty and
inventive-step tests and, for software/algorithms, contribution to a technical effect solving a
technical problem.[^37] A business rule such as "recommend whether to apply" is not automatically a
strong European patent proposition.

Within 90 days, ask specialist counsel for a focused prior-art and patentability review of any truly
technical mechanism in source-change impact propagation, privacy-preserving replay, or integrity-
checked claim compilation. Do this before public technical disclosure if counsel advises. Meanwhile:

- keep policy calibration, incident learning and reviewer operations as controlled trade secrets;
- record invention authorship and design chronology;
- contractually assign employee/contractor IP to the company;
- preserve third-party source and software licence obligations;
- trademark/brand the decision category and trust language if cleared;
- do not hide user facts or obstruct export as a lock-in strategy.

The practical defense is operational learning and trust. A patent, if available, is supplementary.

## Founder-led discovery protocol

Recruit against the behavioral segment, not friends who like the idea. Require a current or recent
cross-border search and ask participants to bring five actual vacancies, their base CV and the
decision they made. Pay for research time; do not condition incentives on positive feedback.

Interview sequence:

1. Reconstruct the last application from vacancy discovery to submit/abandon; do not introduce
   AutoTime yet.
2. Ask where they checked sponsorship, work rights, salary, qualifications and employer identity.
3. Identify the exact point they spent effort before learning a role was unsuitable.
4. Ask them to sort five vacancies into pursue, verify, improve and skip, then explain the evidence.
5. Run the same cases through a concierge AutoTime decision and require an unaided restatement.
6. Insert one deliberately stale or incorrect recommendation and observe whether they challenge it.
7. Ask them to correct a candidate fact and predict how the outcome should change.
8. Prepare one evidence-backed application artifact and time review/correction.
9. Present a real paid offer and checkout; do not ask only "would you pay?"
10. Follow up after 7 and 30 days for action and outcomes.

Capture quotes only with consent, but code behaviors: source used, decision changed, wasted minutes,
unresolved question, correction type, recommendation accepted/rejected, correct restatement, next
action completed, kit approved, application submitted, second role and payment/refund.

Researcher bias controls:

- use a fixed script and rotate explanation variants;
- separate the moderator from the decision reviewer where possible;
- record negative cases and abandonments;
- never help a participant restate before scoring comprehension;
- do not count founder-assisted completion as unassisted activation;
- publish exclusions and missing follow-ups in the internal evidence card.

## Metric contracts

Metrics need denominators, clocks and exclusions before instrumentation:

| Metric                     | Contract                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First-decision completion  | Unique eligible new users receiving a bounded decision / eligible users starting vacancy intake, within 24h                       |
| Correct comprehension      | Participants correctly restating decision, decisive reason and next action without prompts / participants shown decision          |
| Appropriate acceptance     | Correct recommendations accepted / correct recommendations shown                                                                  |
| Error detection            | Deliberately flawed recommendations rejected or corrected / flawed recommendations shown                                          |
| Kit conversion             | `Apply` or overridden decisions reaching user-approved kit / eligible decisions shown                                             |
| Evidence-ready application | User-confirmed submission with reviewed consequential answers, no unsupported claims and linked snapshot / submitted applications |
| Second-role return         | Activated users completing another decision in 28 days / activated users with full observation window                             |
| Correction defect rate     | Corrections classified as extraction/rule/source/country defects / decisions shown                                                |
| Corridor contribution      | Corridor net revenue less attributable variable and allocated governance cost / corridor payers                                   |

Never collapse `Unknown/Investigate` into failure. Track whether the user obtains the missing fact
and returns. Never treat no correction as correctness; it may mean low comprehension or disengagement.

| "Current official sources"            | Only per covered scenario             | Freshness SLA and coverage card visible to user                             |
| "Save application time"               | Unproven                              | Within-user timed baseline, defined workflow and cohort                     |
| "Fewer wasted applications"           | Unproven                              | Prospective baseline and verified skip/abandon rationale                    |
| "More interviews/offers"              | Prohibited                            | Sufficient prospective cohort, baseline/control and reviewed causal wording |
| "Eligible" or "visa-qualified"        | Prohibited pending scope              | Jurisdiction counsel, reviewer authority and scenario evidence              |
| "Works across Europe"                 | Prohibited                            | It is structurally misleading under corridor-specific governance            |

Claims expire when policy, product behavior or evidence changes. Marketing cannot extrapolate a
Germany/Netherlands cohort to the UK/Ireland, all nationalities, all occupations or all job boards.
Outcome reports must disclose observation window, missing outcomes, self-report status, exclusions
and uncertainty.

## Immediate engineering order

1. Add bitemporal mobility source and policy schema.
2. Persist immutable decision snapshots using the canonical contract.
3. Normalize facts and cited spans incrementally from profile JSON.
4. Add material claims and support edges to application artifacts.
5. Add enumerated comprehension, correction, override, approval and outcome events.
6. Build replay, parity, mutation and expiry harnesses.
7. Build expert scenario/sign-off UI after reviewer scope is defined.
8. Add deletion, export, retention and recovery tests for every entity.

Do not implement candidate-specific legal conclusions before regulatory review. Provenance,
versioning, abstention, audit and professional routing remain valuable under every likely decision.

### User-facing moat surfaces

The underlying system should appear as a small number of clear product features:

1. **Role Decision Card** - Apply, Investigate, Improve or Skip; decisive reason first; no false
   precision.
2. **What AutoTime Knows** - verified, declared, inferred, conflicting, stale and unknown facts with
   correction controls.
3. **Official Checks** - source, jurisdiction, reviewed date, coverage limit and direct verification
   route.
4. **What Would Change This** - only reviewed counterfactual conditions and the next fact to obtain.
5. **Claim Ledger** - material application sentences linked to evidence and highlighted when changed
   or awaiting confirmation.
6. **Decision History** - what was shown at the time and, separately, what current policy would say.
7. **Disagree or Correct** - structured reason plus protected optional detail, with preview of impact.
8. **Verify Employer/Offer** - official entity/sponsor state and bounded risk patterns.

These are not eight independent roadmap products. They are views over the same role case, facts,
policy snapshot and claim manifest. A user should reach the Role Decision Card before configuring a
dashboard, tracker, outreach workflow or interview plan.

Exception-based review is the UX principle: verified unchanged facts collapse; inferred, changed,
conflicting, high-risk and unsupported items demand attention. Measure whether this shortens review
without concealing context.

### Repository-to-moat gap map

| Current implementation        | Useful foundation                                                 | Gap to close                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `getDecisionBrief`            | Decision, confidence, risks, missing inputs                       | No immutable snapshot, decisive reason ID, source/policy versions or decision ceiling             |
| `createEvidenceRecords`       | Makes checks visible                                              | Stores copied evidence text, uses coarse status and cannot point to stable source spans or claims |
| `assessCoreLoopTrace`         | Privacy-minimal stage integrity                                   | Analysis history proves presence, not that a particular reviewed decision/version fed the kit     |
| `prepareApplicationKit`       | Separates decision/generator ports and blocks ineligible drafting | Generator receives the original input rather than a signed decision/evidence manifest             |
| `mobility_profiles` JSON      | Consent-aware persistence with RLS                                | No atomic fact identity, conflict, supersession, provenance or selective deletion                 |
| `workflow_operational_events` | Idempotent server-side funnel events                              | Only four stages; no comprehension, correction, override, approval or outcome semantics           |

The safest migration path is dual-write, verify, then read-switch:

1. Create additive source, fact, policy and snapshot tables with RLS and cascade tests.
2. On a decision, write both current history and the new immutable snapshot.
3. Compare normalized current and replayed decisions in shadow mode.
4. Backfill no legal conclusions; migrate only facts with unambiguous provenance.
5. Switch application preparation to a decision snapshot ID plus evidence manifest.
6. Retire copied evidence text only after export, deletion and support workflows use stable IDs.

Do not turn the existing profile JSON into a universal event store. Keep it as a compatibility
projection while normalized facts become the governed source for decisions.

### Minimal persistence blueprint

This is a design target, not an approved migration:

| Entity                  | Essential fields                                                | Privacy/immutability rule                                   |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `role_cases`            | user, vacancy, corridor, lifecycle                              | User-owned; delete cascade                                  |
| `evidence_sources`      | user, kind, encrypted object reference, content hash            | Raw content access logged; delete cascade                   |
| `evidence_facts`        | semantic type, normalized value, status, valid time, supersedes | Append/supersede; never elevate without user/reviewer event |
| `source_spans`          | evidence source, locator, span hash                             | Locator must revalidate against source hash                 |
| `mobility_sources`      | publisher, URL, jurisdiction, licence/attribution               | No candidate data                                           |
| `source_revisions`      | content hash, retrieved/reviewed times, prior revision          | Immutable shared operational record                         |
| `policy_rule_versions`  | typed predicate, valid/system time, revision, review state      | Publish only through signed review transition               |
| `decision_snapshots`    | input manifest hash, rule set, result, ceiling, timestamp       | Immutable; user-owned link, shared rule refs                |
| `decision_reasons`      | snapshot, fact/rule refs, severity, recourse                    | No copied raw content                                       |
| `application_artifacts` | snapshot, version, approval state, content object               | User-owned; immutable approved version                      |
| `material_claims`       | artifact, text hash, location, risk class                       | Claim text protected as service data                        |
| `claim_support`         | claim, fact/span, support type, review state                    | Referential integrity blocks orphan approval                |
| `corrections_overrides` | target ID, enum reason, old/new hashes, actor                   | Free-text separately protected; immutable audit event       |

Use separate schemas or explicit privilege boundaries for user evidence, shared mobility knowledge,
research datasets and analytics. Service-role access is not a substitute for least privilege. Every
table must have an owner, retention class, export representation, deletion behavior and backup-
recovery test before production.

### Canonical decision contract v1

```json
{
  "schema_version": 1,
  "decision": "investigate",
  "decision_ceiling": "investigate",
  "decisive_reason_id": "reason_uuid",
  "hard_blockers": [],
  "soft_gaps": [],
  "unknowns": ["employing_legal_entity"],
  "positive_evidence": [],
  "next_action": {
    "type": "verify_employer_entity",
    "source_revision_id": "uuid"
  },
  "confidence": { "band": "bounded", "basis": "decisive_unknown" },
  "evidence_manifest_hash": "sha256",
  "policy_bundle_id": "uuid",
  "source_revision_ids": ["uuid"],
  "generated_at": "ISO-8601"
}
```

Confidence is a band with a reason, not an uncalibrated percentage. Hashes detect drift but do not
replace retained manifests and records. The decision ceiling is evaluated after factual rules and
before user-facing wording, so a prohibited conclusion cannot leak through an explanation.

## Operational and experimental R&D

### Source operations design

Source maintenance must operate like a production dependency with service levels, not editorial
content. UK sponsor data can change repeatedly within a week and is published as a large CSV; the
Dutch register states a monthly update cadence.[^26][^27] A uniform quarterly review would therefore
be unsafe for the UK and wasteful for slower sources.

| Source class              | Poll/review trigger                                   | Automated action                                     | Human action                                      |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Sponsor registers         | Match publisher cadence; daily for UK, monthly for NL | Fetch, hash, schema validate, entity diff            | Review schema/anomaly and disputed entities       |
| Salary/occupation tables  | Daily page change check plus scheduled annual review  | DOM/text diff, affected-rule lookup                  | Interpret effective date and approve typed values |
| Statute/rules             | Change notification plus daily metadata check         | Store immutable revision; freeze affected conclusion | Qualified legal/policy review                     |
| Explanatory guidance      | Weekly change check                                   | Diff relevant sections and flag citations            | Decide whether rule or explanation changed        |
| Product research evidence | Quarterly literature watch                            | Deduplicate and tag claims                           | Update experiment, not production legal rules     |

Ingestion states should be `detected`, `parsed`, `review_required`, `approved`, `rejected`,
`published`, `superseded` and `incident`. Detection never publishes a rule. A schema failure or
unreviewed critical change automatically downgrades affected output to unknown/investigate.

Sponsor lookup requires entity resolution, not lowercase string equality. A vacancy brand can differ
from the employing legal entity; similarly named entities can have different registrations. Show
`verified exact identifier`, `probable name match`, `ambiguous`, `not found` and `stale source` as
distinct states. Only an official identifier or user/employer confirmation may produce the first.

The source pipeline needs fixtures for removed sponsors, renamed entities, duplicate trading names,
blank identifiers, malformed rows, source outage, retroactive correction and a changed file format.
Measure detection-to-safe-downgrade and detection-to-reviewed-publication separately.

Maintain a source-rights register alongside source freshness. ESCO's API software has stated EUPL
1.2 licensing, EURES authorises content reuse with attribution subject to document-specific terms,
and GOV.UK reuse generally requires Open Government Licence compliance and attribution.[^28][^29][^30]
Do not assume every government page, database, logo or third-party attachment has identical terms.
For each source record store licence URL/version, attribution text, permitted use, extraction method,
terms-review date and any database-right restriction. This matters if the knowledge layer becomes a
commercial asset.

### Bet A: selective decisioning

Research a decision policy that abstains on the smallest decisive unknown instead of returning a
weak overall answer. The product should ask one high-information question at a time and measure
coverage against critical-error cost.

- Hypothesis: targeted abstention reduces false certainty without materially reducing completion.
- Prototype: deterministic rule dependency graph plus `unknown -> next best fact` resolver.
- Measures: coverage, false green, unsafe certainty, questions per decision, abandonment.
- Success: zero critical false greens and at least 70% of initiated cases reach a bounded outcome.
- Kill: users encounter repeated unknowns with no actionable verification route.

### Bet B: verified counterfactual recourse

Show the smallest factual change that would alter the decision, such as employer sponsorship
confirmation, but distinguish a hypothetical from an action the user can cause. Research cautions
that mathematically minimal recourse does not necessarily predict what people accept or do.[^31]

- Hypothesis: source-linked counterfactuals improve comprehension and next-action completion.
- Prototype: rule dependency traversal, not model-generated feature importance.
- Measures: correct restatement, action completion, mistaken belief that hypothetical is probable.
- Success: 15-point comprehension lift with no increase in unsafe reliance.
- Kill: users treat counterfactual conditions as promises or cannot act on them.

### Bet C: source-change blast radius

On a source revision, identify affected rules, test cases, historical decisions and active users
without exposing raw candidate content to reviewers.

- Hypothesis: dependency edges cut rule-change response from days to hours.
- Prototype: source revision -> rule version -> scenario/decision identifiers.
- Measures: time to detection, affected-case recall, false alerts, time to reviewed publication.
- Success: 100% planted critical cases identified in a quarterly change drill.
- Kill: manual corridor review is faster and equally reliable at the supported scale.

### Bet D: evidence compression with reversibility

Convert repeated CV/profile material into atomic facts with cited spans, while retaining the source
and enough context to reconstruct meaning.

- Hypothesis: fact reuse lowers tokens and review time without increasing omissions/conflicts.
- Measures: duplicate rate, correction propagation, token cost, source-span validity, expert loss.
- Success: 30% lower generation input cost and faster review with no material integrity regression.
- Kill: atomization repeatedly loses qualifications, chronology or nuanced responsibility.

### Bet E: corridor reliability score

Do not publish one confidence score. Internally calculate corridor readiness from source freshness,
scenario coverage, reviewer agreement, incident severity and production correction rate.

- Hypothesis: readiness gates predict where errors occur better than country availability flags.
- Measures: incident rate by readiness band, calibration, release blocks and false alarms.
- Success: no critical incident in the highest readiness band across two release cycles.
- Kill: the score becomes a subjective checklist that cannot predict failures.

### Bet F: adviser co-pilot packet

Prepare structured facts and questions for a regulated adviser without supplying a prohibited
eligibility conclusion.

- Hypothesis: advisers can review a case faster and return higher-quality structured corrections.
- Measures: adviser minutes, missing-fact rate, correction yield, referral conversion, margin.
- Success: 30% less preparation time and positive unit contribution in a 20-case pilot.
- Kill: advisers must redo extraction or will not accept provenance/consent controls.

### Scenario ontology v1

The evaluation corpus should cross these axes rather than accumulate convenient examples:

| Axis             | Required initial values                                                                      |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Work-right state | no sponsorship needed, sponsorship required, existing permission, unknown, conflicting       |
| Employer state   | verified sponsor, not listed, ambiguous entity, employer claims sponsorship, stale registry  |
| Compensation     | clearly above, boundary, below, missing, non-comparable period/currency                      |
| Occupation       | exact code, competing codes, duties contradict title, regulated profession, unclassified     |
| Qualification    | recognised, plausibly related, unrelated, absent, recognition pending                        |
| Vacancy language | explicit support, explicit rejection, generic equal-opportunity text, silence, contradiction |
| Evidence state   | verified, declared, inferred, stale, conflicting, missing                                    |
| Source state     | current, expired, changed but unreviewed, unavailable, conflicting official sources          |
| Decision         | Apply, Investigate, Improve, Skip                                                            |
| Fairness stress  | career gap, foreign institution, transliteration, disability accommodation, atypical path    |

Generate pairwise coverage first, then add targeted three-way interactions around hard blockers.
For every scenario, define decisive facts, expected outcome, acceptable alternative, prohibited
outcome, rule/source versions and counterfactual mutations. Mutation tests should remove, reverse or
expire each decisive input and assert the expected decision delta.

### Research operations and evidence quality

Every study must store: hypothesis, decision it informs, protocol version, recruitment source,
sample and exclusions, consent version, raw-data retention, analysis, result, limitations, decision
owner and decision date. Negative results remain discoverable.

Evidence grades:

| Grade | Meaning                                                      | Permitted decision                   |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| E0    | Opinion or untested assumption                               | Discovery only                       |
| E1    | Moderated qualitative pattern                                | Prototype/iterate                    |
| E2    | Repeated behavior or paid action in a narrow cohort          | Controlled product decision          |
| E3    | Held-out evaluation or comparative experiment                | Release/scale within tested boundary |
| E4    | Replicated cohort/outcome evidence with reviewed methodology | Marketing or expansion consideration |

Feature completion never exceeds E0 for customer value. A payment without product use validates
checkout and some intent, not sustained value. An interview outcome without a baseline and cohort
definition cannot validate causation.

### Assurance artifacts per release

Use the NIST AI RMF cycle - govern, map, measure and manage - as an operating vocabulary, not a
compliance badge. NIST emphasizes repeatable evaluation, uncertainty, documented limits,
independent review, end-user feedback and tracking risks over time.[^35] For every decision-policy
release publish internally:

- intended purpose, prohibited uses and supported corridors/scenarios;
- model/provider and prompt versions used for extraction/generation;
- policy bundle and source revisions;
- locked-corpus results by corridor and scenario family;
- critical errors, abstention coverage and cross-surface parity;
- human comprehension and appropriate-reliance evidence;
- expert reviewer scope/agreement and unresolved disagreement;
- privacy/data-use changes and DPIA delta;
- production corrections/incidents since prior release;
- rollback owner, trigger and last successful recovery exercise.

NIST's generative-AI profile treats confident false content and even fabricated logic/citations as
a consequential confabulation risk.[^36] Accordingly, an LLM-generated citation is never provenance.
Only a source revision retrieved and validated by the governed pipeline can support a mobility rule.
