# Market and Commercial Model

**Status:** evidence-backed sizing framework; revenue forecast remains unvalidated.

## What the available evidence supports

The EU employed 10.45 million ICT specialists in 2025, 5.0% of total employment. Germany represented
2.3 million, France 1.4 million, Spain just over 1 million and Italy about 0.9 million.[^1] This proves
a large technology labour base, not AutoTime's serviceable market.

EU states issued 78,096 EU Blue Cards in 2024. Germany issued 56,252 (72.0%), followed by Poland
5,853, France 2,775 and Spain 1,924 among the leading issuers.[^2] This strongly supports Germany as a
research beachhead, but Blue Cards omit national skilled routes—including the Netherlands Highly
Skilled Migrant scheme—and do not identify technology applicants alone.

The EU issued 3.5 million first residence permits in 2024, with employment accounting for 31.9%.[^3]
This is an outer labour-migration context, not an addressable-market count. Product sizing must not
mix permits, people, applications, vacancies and subscriptions.

Eurostat's experimental online-job-ad data indicates that 8.5% of online advertisements from Q4 2022
through Q3 2023 sought ICT specialists; 59.1% of ICT ads sought software/application developers and
analysts.[^4] The dataset warns that advertisements are not equivalent to vacancies and may be
duplicated, unfilled or cover multiple positions.[^5]

## Bottom-up sizing equation

For each origin × destination × occupation × quarter cohort:

```text
serviceable decisions
= unique sponsor-addressable tech vacancies
 × share visible to non-EU candidates
 × share with sufficient decision inputs
 × reachable-intent share

paying customers
= unique candidates making serviceable decisions
 × activation rate
 × paid conversion

net revenue
= paying customers × realised ARPPU
- refunds - payment taxes/fees

contribution
= net revenue
- inference - source operations - expert review - support - incident/correction cost
```

Every input must carry low/base/high values, provenance and observation date. Until real cohorts
exist, conversion, repeat use and ARPPU are assumptions—not market facts.

## Market-priority evidence dimensions

| Dimension              | Evidence                                 | Why it matters                           | Current limitation                                |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Skilled-migration flow | Route permits and first permits          | Reveals actual historical movement       | Does not equal application intent or tech roles   |
| Tech labour base       | ICT specialist employment                | Indicates employer ecosystem depth       | Mostly incumbent workers, not open vacancies      |
| Tech hiring intensity  | Online job ads and hard-to-fill surveys  | Indicates active demand/mismatch         | Experimental, lagged and not sponsorship-labelled |
| Rule determinism       | Official thresholds/lists/registers      | Controls automation feasibility          | Administrative judgement still matters            |
| Sponsor observability  | Official employer registers              | Enables a differentiated verified signal | Entity matching and list history are non-trivial  |
| Acquisition reach      | Search, communities, referrals, partners | Controls obtainable demand               | No measured CAC yet                               |
| Willingness to pay     | Real checkout behaviour                  | Validates business value                 | Not yet observed                                  |
| Service cost           | Model, support, research and experts     | Controls contribution margin             | No production cohort ledger                       |

## Commercial experiments

### Experiment A — paid decision pack

- Audience: non-EU software/data/security professionals actively considering Germany or Netherlands.
- Unit: five real vacancy assessments with source-linked reasons and evidence gaps.
- Test prices: £19, £39 and £59 across comparable recruitment channels.
- Primary metric: completed payment, not stated preference.
- Secondary metrics: activation, assessment completion, refund, repeat pack and support minutes.
- Failure: fewer than three payments after 30 qualified prospects at each viable channel/offer cell.

### Experiment B — monthly evidence workspace

- Offer: reusable verified facts, sponsor checks, decision history and application claim controls.
- Test price: £29–£49/month.
- Retention signal: second-month payment plus at least three distinct vacancy decisions.
- Failure: users consume one decision and churn; this implies transactional rather than subscription
  value.

### Experiment C — reviewed corridor packet

- Offer: software-generated evidence packet reviewed by a properly authorised expert within a defined
  scope.
- Test price: £99–£249 excluding government and third-party fees.
- Measure: expert minutes, correction types, margin and customer reliance—not only conversion.
- Constraint: do not sell before the regulatory arrangement and reviewer scope are documented.

## Unit-economics acceptance gates

Do not call the model viable until a cohort ledger proves:

- realised gross receipts and refunds;
- Stripe fees and taxes;
- inference and storage cost per active customer;
- source-operations time by market;
- expert-review and escalation minutes;
- support and correction cost;
- acquisition cost by channel;
- repeat-use/retention by cohort;
- contribution margin after all variable and semi-variable delivery costs.

A target contribution margin can guide design, but only observed costs validate it. Country expansion
must be refused when incremental source/expert/support cost exceeds realistic incremental revenue.

## Present portfolio inference

Germany is the strongest evidence-backed entry market because it combines dominant Blue Card volume
with the EU's largest ICT workforce. The Netherlands remains paired because sponsor verification is
product-differentiating even though Blue Card totals do not represent its main route. France and
Spain have larger ICT workforces than several higher-ranked markets but add route/threshold
complexity. Sweden, Luxembourg and Finland have high ICT shares but smaller absolute pools. These are
portfolio hypotheses; paid acquisition and retention evidence can overturn them.
