## 0. What changed in this deeper pass — read this first

If you only read one section, read this one. The first pass under-researched five things the founder specifically flagged, and two of the new findings materially change the risk picture:

1. **Jobbatical itself is a graveyard signal, not just a content competitor.** Jobbatical was founded in 2014 as a candidate-facing international recruitment platform — structurally similar in spirit to LandWell's original ambition — and discovered the real problem wasn't matching candidates to jobs, it was immigration logistics. It pivoted in mid-2019 to become a **B2B relocation-logistics SaaS for employer HR/mobility teams**, abandoning the candidate-facing model entirely. (§1.2)
2. **Localyze, the other named "B2B, not really a competitor" player, was acquired in October 2025** by the US firm Boundless Immigration — the European candidate-facing/B2B mobility space is consolidating into larger employer-side platforms, not spawning new candidate-facing entrants. (§1.2)
3. **Real willingness-to-pay benchmarks now exist** (§4): UK immigration lawyer initial consultations run £50–£360; a formal visa eligibility assessment/review runs £200–£500; LinkedIn Premium Career runs $20–40/month; JobPing (the closest AI-native comparable, see below) prices its own visa-sponsorship-filter feature at **€7/month**. This gives a plausible price ceiling and floor for LandWell's own pilot pricing that the first pass had zero data on.
4. **Real demand-side numbers now exist for all four original corridors, not just two.** Netherlands HSM arrivals fell from 26,000 (2022) to 14,000 (2025) — a 46% decline, previously undocumented. Ireland issued 31,044 total employment permits in 2025 (up from 39,390 gross issuances in 2024 once refusals are netted differently — see caveat in §3). (§3)
5. **Two new corridors (France, and Denmark/Sweden) are now researched to the same depth as the original four**, including a genuinely new regulatory finding: Sweden's work-permit salary threshold jumped materially on 1 June 2026 (though IT specialists are exempted at a lower bar), and the UK's Immigration Salary List — flagged in the first pass as "if it still exists" — is confirmed to still exist but is scheduled to be **abolished entirely on 31 December 2026**, replaced by a stricter Temporary Shortage List. (§2.6, §6)

## 1. Competitor / market gap analysis

### 1.1 The landscape, by category (retained and lightly corrected from the first pass)

**A. Visa-filtered tech job boards**
- **Relocate.me** — the closest direct competitor in spirit. Hand-curates tech jobs (software engineers, DevOps, cybersecurity) explicitly tagged with visa sponsorship and relocation support; manually reviews and rewrites every listing before publishing. Founded 2017. Free tier plus a paid tier (~100 hand-picked jobs/week, resume review, recruiter Q&A). As of this research, ~1,235 open roles, ~992 tagged with relocation/visa sponsorship. **Traction signal, new this pass:** Relocate.me has essentially **no G2 review presence** — G2's own listing for it says "there are not enough reviews... for G2 to provide buying insight," and the vendor profile hasn't been actively managed "for over a year" per G2's own note. That's a real, if soft, signal that Relocate.me's traction (at least in the B2B review-site sense) is thin, despite being the most name-checked candidate-facing player in this space. ([Relocate.me](https://relocate.me/blog/working-abroad/visa-sponsorship-jobs-in-europe/); [G2 Relocate.me reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/relocate-me/reviews); [jobboardsearch.com review](https://jobboardsearch.com/job-boards/relocate-me), accessed 16 Sept 2026)
  - **What it does not do:** it is a curated *listing* filter, not a decision engine. It tells you a company/role is tagged "sponsors visas" — it does not assess whether *your specific profile* clears a specific country's salary/occupation threshold, whether the listed employer is even the legal sponsoring entity, or what the mobility risk is for that particular vacancy.
- **StepStone, Indeed Europe, LinkedIn** — general job boards with "visa sponsorship"/"relocation" keyword filters, self-reported/inferred, not verified, no fit or eligibility assessment. Confirmed by search: "Job boards like LinkedIn and Indeed lack high-level categorization clearly stating whether visa sponsorship is available or not" is a recurring user complaint.
- **EURES** (eures.europa.eu) — the EU's own official cross-border jobs portal. Pure listing aggregator, no fit scoring, no eligibility assessment.
- **arbeitnow.com** — plain job board, no visa filtering, used for the existing case corpus.

**B. AI-native "sponsorship checker / job-matching" tools — this pass found materially more of these than the first pass did**

This category turned out to be denser than the first pass suggested. A harder search (Product Hunt, Chrome Web Store, direct product-name searches) surfaced a cluster of small, mostly US/H-1B-oriented tools that are the closest thing to a structural precedent for what LandWell is trying to build, even though none of them are Europe-native or do per-vacancy mobility-risk scoring:

- **JobPing** (getjobping.com) — the most directly comparable tool found in this entire research pass, and the only one with a transparent, checkable pricing page. Scans 4,000+ career pages/job boards daily across the EU and UK; curates graduate/entry-level roles into ~9 career-path categories (Strategy, Data, Product, Finance, Marketing, etc.); includes a "CV Ping" feature that scores CV-to-JD fit with bullet-point rewrite suggestions. **Pricing (fetched directly from getjobping.com/pricing, 16 Sept 2026): Free tier = €0, one-time 10 matches, 3 lifetime CV analyses, 1 career path, 30-day-old listings only. Premium tier = €7/month, cancel anytime — 15 curated matches/week (Mon/Wed/Fri), 2 career paths, unlimited CV analyses, listings within 7 days, and — notably — visa sponsorship filtering is a Premium-only, paid feature, not free.** That is a direct, load-bearing data point: a comparable product already prices its sponsorship-filter capability as a paid add-on at €7/month, which is a real anchor for LandWell's own pricing discussion, not a proxy. **Traction signals (displayed live on the pricing page):** "16.1k Active Roles This Week," "757+ Companies Scanned This Week," "21 Cities EU & UK This Week" — self-reported, not independently audited, but at minimum shows JobPing is a live, actively-marketed product with a real (if early-career/generalist) user base, not a dead or hypothetical one. ([getjobping.com/pricing](https://www.getjobping.com/pricing), fetched directly 16 Sept 2026)
  - JobPing's own July 2026 database data (cited in the first pass, reconfirmed): only **2.6% of tech job postings explicitly flag sponsorship** (vs. 5% for finance), with Poland (8.8%), Ireland (5.8%), Spain (4.2%) the highest early-career flag rates. ([getjobping.com blog, 14 July 2026](https://www.getjobping.com/blog/international-graduate-visa-sponsorship-2026))
  - **What it does not do for Europe specifically:** it is a pass/fail sponsorship filter layered on top of a general graduate job-matching product, not a mobility-risk or salary-threshold-fit assessment against a specific corridor's current legal rules. It doesn't compute "does this candidate + this vacancy clear Ireland's €40,904 threshold."
- **MySponza** ("The AI Co-Pilot For Your Job Hunt") — a CV/application/interview-prep companion; positioning suggests visa-sponsorship awareness but the public site (fetched directly) gives essentially no product detail, no pricing, and no traction numbers — thin enough that it reads as an early-stage or pre-traction product. ([mysponza.com](https://www.mysponza.com/), fetched 16 Sept 2026)
- **Sponsorly** (sponsorly.dev / sponsorly.app) — a free Chrome extension that checks visa sponsorship on LinkedIn/Workday/Greenhouse job pages in real time and tailors a resume with AI (built on Llama 3.3 70B + "real H1B data"). US H-1B-focused. Its own site states subscription plans are still "coming soon" as of this research — i.e., it is pre-monetization. ([sponsorly.dev](https://sponsorly.dev/), [Chrome Web Store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/), accessed 16 Sept 2026)
- **FrogHire.ai** — free Chrome extension surfacing H-1B sponsor history on job boards. US-focused, browser-extension-only, no fit/risk scoring.
- **UK Visa Sponsorship Checker** (Chrome Web Store) — genuinely the closest UK-specific precedent found: an AI-powered extension that, when browsing LinkedIn/Indeed, shows whether a company holds a sponsor licence, the exact registered sponsor name, whether the specific role type has been sponsored historically, and how many visas that employer has sponsored. This is meaningfully closer to LandWell's ambition than anything else found — it's UK-specific, per-listing, and grounded in the actual UK sponsor register — but it is a browser extension surfacing historical sponsor data, not a per-candidate salary/occupation-threshold fit calculator, and it doesn't cover Ireland, Germany, Netherlands, France, or the Nordics. ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/uk-visa-sponsorship-check/jjdlecgjgcejnobmljdmjolnadeplapb), accessed 16 Sept 2026)
- **JobMetasearch.ai, Scale.jobs, migratemate.co-listed tools** — a longer tail of AI job-metasearch tools that layer visa-sponsorship filtering onto general job aggregation. All found via this pass's harder Product Hunt/search-term sweep; none reviewed in the first pass. None appear to be Europe-specific decision engines; most are US-market-first with a Europe filter bolted on.
- **MyVisaJobs, USponsorMe** — US H-1B/green-card sponsorship-history databases, reconfirmed US-only, no European equivalent found in either pass.

**C. Employer-side immigration/relocation platforms — one major consolidation event found this pass**
- **Localyze — acquired by Boundless Immigration on 1 October 2025.** This is new, and materially changes how "Localyze" should be cited as a competitor: it is no longer an independent European startup but part of a larger US-headquartered immigration platform (Boundless's third acquisition, after Bridge in 2023 and RapidVisa in 2020). Localyze itself was YC-backed (2019 batch), General-Catalyst-led Series B of $35M (Sept 2022), **total raised ~$47.3M across 4 rounds from 17 investors** per PitchBook/Crunchbase. Berlin-based, launched 2018. The acquisition was explicitly framed around US immigration-system stress ("Amid Trump immigration crackdown, Seattle startup Boundless acquires European competitor" — GeekWire's own headline) — i.e., a US company buying European mobility-tech capability to serve US enterprise clients moving people globally, not a sign of organic growth in European candidate-facing demand. ([Boundless press release, 1 Oct 2025](https://www.boundless.com/press/boundless-acquires-european-mobility-leader-localyze-creating-a-unified-solution-to-navigate-global-workforce-challenges); [GeekWire, 1 Oct 2025](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/amid-trump-immigration-crackdown-seattles-boundless-acquires-european-competitor/); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/279630-10))
- **Deel Immigration** — EOR platform bundling payroll/compliance with immigration support in 30–70+ countries (sources vary on exact count). **Pricing confirmed as fully custom/quote-based, no public rate card** — the product surfaces a visa-eligibility check with a "results within 48 hours" turnaround and cost breakdown before a company commits to a hire, which is itself an interesting UX precedent (fast, structured eligibility pre-check) worth studying even though it's B2B-only. ([deel.com/hr-services/employee-immigration](https://www.deel.com/hr-services/employee-immigration/), [HireSwiftlee pricing breakdown](https://hireswiftlee.com/blog/deel-pricing-explained), accessed 16 Sept 2026)
- **What neither does:** these are entirely employer-side, engaged after a hire decision, with zero candidate-facing "should I apply" function. As previously concluded, a plausible future LandWell distribution channel is partnering with, not competing against, this layer.

**D. Relocation consultancies / immigration law firms** — unchanged from the first pass; see §4 for the new pricing data extracted from this category.

**E. Community advice (Reddit, Blind/teamblind.com)** — unchanged; corroborating evidence, not a competitor.

### 1.2 The graveyard search — the specific new finding the founder asked for

A direct "shut down" / "ceased operations" search for this category surfaced fewer literal startup deaths than expected, but the two most important findings are structural pivots, which are arguably more informative than an outright shutdown for LandWell's specific model:

1. **Jobbatical's own history is the strongest cautionary precedent found in this research.** Jobbatical launched in 2014 in Estonia as exactly the kind of candidate-facing international recruitment/matching platform LandWell's early framing resembles — "a community of borderless, highly skilled people" introduced to founders and companies. Per Sifted's own reporting, the company **learned the biggest problem facing employers was not hiring the talent but getting them into the country** — i.e., discovered that job-matching wasn't the binding constraint, immigration logistics was. It pivoted in **July 2019** to build a technology platform purely around the immigration/relocation process, re-launching in Spain, Germany, and Estonia by May 2020, and by 2020 had grown relocation-services volume **365% year-on-year** — but only after abandoning the candidate-facing matching model for a B2B relocation-logistics SaaS sold to employer mobility teams. It later raised €11.6M in a 2022 round explicitly framed around "worker migration goes paperless" (TechCrunch). ([Sifted, "Immigration made easy: Jobbatical's big plans for startup talent"](https://sifted.eu/articles/talent-relocation-jobbatical); [TechCrunch, 9 Sept 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/09/jobbatical-rasies-e11-6m-as-worker-migration-goes-paperless/))
   - **Why this matters for LandWell specifically:** Jobbatical is not a company that failed and vanished — it's a company that tried the candidate-facing "help you find and win a sponsored job" model first, found the real pain and real willingness-to-pay was on the employer/logistics side instead, and pivoted its whole business model to chase it. That is a direct, dated precedent for the exact risk the mission plan's §10 payment-gate question is trying to de-risk: it is entirely possible that "which vacancy should I apply to" is not, in fact, where the money is, and that the real paying need sits one step downstream (post-offer logistics), where Jobbatical, Localyze, and Deel already compete. This should be treated as the single most important competitive-risk finding of this whole research pass, not a footnote.
2. **Localyze's 2025 acquisition (§1.1) is a second consolidation signal in the same direction** — an independent, candidate-adjacent European mobility startup ending up folded into a larger US employer-side platform rather than continuing to grow independently or expanding toward candidates.
3. **No outright candidate-facing "visa sponsorship job platform" shutdown was found with a public "we're shutting down" post** in either research pass, despite deliberately targeted search terms ("visa sponsorship startup shut down," "relocation platform failed," "we're shutting down immigration tech"). The one confirmed shutdown found, JobisJob, was a generalist multi-country job aggregator with no visa-sponsorship specialization, so it's weak evidence either way. **Read this as an absence of positive evidence, not a clean bill of health** — the category may simply be too young and too small (most of the AI-native tools in §1.1.B are single-founder, pre-seed, or pre-monetization) to have produced a documented failure yet, rather than proof the model is safe.

### 1.3 Honest verdict on the gap (retained, with one addition)

The specific gap LandWell claims — per-vacancy fit + mobility risk assessment before applying — still appears genuinely unaddressed by any competitor found across both research passes, for the European market specifically. The **UK Visa Sponsorship Checker** Chrome extension (§1.1.B, new this pass) is the closest thing found to a live, shipping, candidate-facing, per-listing tool grounded in real sponsor data — worth a direct hands-on trial before finalizing LandWell's UK country-pack UX, since it may already have solved part of the "is this listing real" sub-problem for one corridor.

The three qualifications from the first pass still hold (Jobbatical's static content is a real information-layer competitor; Relocate.me's curation is a soft substitute; no direct evidence of willingness to pay specifically for the fit/risk layer as distinct from listing curation). Add a fourth, new to this pass:

4. **The candidate-facing "sponsorship intelligence" category is small, young, and thin on paid traction.** Of the ~7 AI-native tools surfaced in §1.1.B, only JobPing has a public, live pricing page with real (if self-reported) usage numbers; the rest are either content-free landing pages (MySponza), pre-monetization (Sponsorly), or free browser extensions with no visible business model at all (FrogHire, UK Visa Sponsorship Checker). This is double-edged: it means there's real white space, but it also means nobody in this specific niche has yet proven a durable paid business model at any scale — LandWell would be one of the first to actually try to charge for this exact thing, not entering a proven-but-crowded market.

## 3. Deeper demand-side research

### 3.1 Corridor-by-corridor demand direction — now four of six corridors have real numbers (was two of four)

- **UK: shrinking, strong primary-adjacent evidence.** (unchanged from first pass, see §2.3)

**Synthesis, and what it means for the pilot payment gate:**

1. **A plausible LandWell candidate-facing price ceiling, based on direct comparables, is roughly €5–15/month or a one-time fee in the low tens of euros** — anchored by JobPing's €7/month (a genuinely comparable feature, not just an analogous career tool) and LinkedIn Premium's $20/month floor. Pricing meaningfully above that (e.g., a £200+ one-time fee matching a lawyer's formal assessment) would require LandWell to credibly position itself as a *substitute for*, not a *cheaper triage layer before*, a paid legal consultation — a much higher bar of trust and accuracy than a pilot is likely to have earned.
2. **A one-time "per-vacancy assessment" fee in the €5–20 range is a defensible test point**, sitting well below even the cheapest lawyer consultation (£50) while still being a real payment, not a token amount — this directly informs what number to actually test at the priced-pilot stage (mission plan step 6).
3. **The employer-paid relocation-package numbers ($15k–$75k+) are useful context but not a pricing anchor for LandWell** — they represent a completely different buyer (employer, post-offer) and transaction size than a candidate deciding whether to spend a few euros pre-application. Citing them as "look how much this market is worth" in founder-facing materials would be a category error; they should stay clearly labeled as B2B/post-offer context only, as this section does.
4. **No consumer SaaS product found in either research pass prices a candidate-facing visa/sponsorship eligibility check on its own, standalone, at any price point.** JobPing bundles it into a broader career-matching subscription; Sponsorly and MySponza are free or pre-monetization; the UK Visa Sponsorship Checker extension gives no pricing at all. This means **LandWell would be pricing a genuinely new standalone SKU**, not benchmarking against an established comparable's stated price for the same specific thing — the €7/month and £200–500 figures above are the best available anchors, but both require a real analogy, not a direct match.

## What this changes about the plan (updated)

Nothing here changes the mission plan's core sequencing — the founder-interview bottleneck remains the actual next action. But this deeper pass sharpens the inputs further than the first pass did:

- **The Jobbatical pivot and Localyze acquisition together are the strongest reason yet to treat the candidate-facing "should I apply" layer as an unproven business model, not just an unclaimed niche.** This doesn't mean don't build it — it means the founder-interview questions in `slice-selection-interview-kit.md` should specifically probe whether candidates would pay *before* applying (LandWell's bet) or only value help *after* getting an offer (where Jobbatical/Localyze/Deel already compete and where the money has historically gone).
- **A concrete price point is now testable**, not just a research gap: something in the **€5–20 one-time-fee or €5–10/month range**, anchored against JobPing's €7/month and comfortably below any lawyer-consultation price, is the number to actually put in front of pilot users at mission-plan step 6, rather than guessing.
- **The Netherlands should be re-scored alongside the UK as a higher-mobility-risk corridor**, not treated as a safe fallback if the UK looks too volatile — both now have hard double-digit-percentage demand declines, just on different timeframes.
- **The UK's 31 December 2026 ISL sunset is a concrete date to build a content-freshness checkpoint around**, regardless of which corridor is ultimately chosen for the pilot — it's a real test of whether the country-pack maintenance process actually catches a scheduled, publicly announced change before it goes stale.
- **France, Sweden, and Denmark are now viable candidates for corridor-selection scoring on the same evidentiary footing as the original four**, though all three still lack volume/trend data and pending-reform screening as thorough as the UK/Netherlands got in this pass — a reasonable next research increment if any of them becomes a live candidate.
