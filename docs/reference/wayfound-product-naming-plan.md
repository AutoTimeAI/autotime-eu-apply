## 1. Decision summary

`Wayfound` is the preferred candidate name for the customer-facing product.
It is not yet an approved production rename.

The founder's comfort using the name as the product's first user is a strong
qualitative signal because founder conviction affects how consistently the
product is explained and sold. It is not, by itself, evidence that the name is
legally available, understood by target users, memorable, pronounceable, or
commercially effective.

The naming decision must therefore follow two separate stages:

1. **Validate Wayfound as a brand.** Complete legal, linguistic, market,
   customer and technical due diligence.
2. **Rename the customer-facing product only after a recorded GO decision.**
   Preserve the company, repository and internal infrastructure names unless a
   later decision explicitly changes them.

The working brand proposition is:

> **Wayfound**  
> Evidence-backed decisions for cross-border technology careers.

The working customer promise is:

> Find the European technology opportunities worth your time, understand the
> evidence, and choose your next move with greater clarity.

## 2. Why this naming work exists

The current name creates several incorrect or incomplete expectations:

- `AutoTime` correctly expresses the business idea of saving time through
  automation, but does not independently communicate this product's decision
  and evidence qualities.
- `EU` technically means the 27-member European Union, while the planned
  product boundary is 32 markets: EU 27, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein,
  Switzerland and the United Kingdom.
- `Apply` can make the product sound like an autofill or mass-application tool.
- The implemented product is richer: it combines job-fit decisions, mobility
  evidence, provenance, application preparation, outcome tracking and learning.

`Wayfound` is intended to create a distinct, human brand around finding a
defensible path. The product descriptor—not the name alone—must explain the
category.

## 3. Naming principles

The selected name must:

1. Be memorable and pronounceable without a large advertising budget.
2. Support a serious, trust-sensitive product without sounding like legal
   advice or a government service.
3. Avoid implying automatic submission, guaranteed employment, visa approval
   or immigration eligibility.
4. Work across the full 32-market European scope without using `EU` as a
   geographic shortcut.
5. Remain useful if the product later expands beyond Europe.
6. Accommodate job decisions, mobility, evidence, preparation and learning.
7. Be sufficiently distinctive in relevant software, careers, recruitment,
   education, immigration and mobility categories.
8. Be usable by a solo bootstrapper: easy to explain, visually express and
   deploy incrementally.

## 4. Proposed brand architecture

### 4.1 Initial public architecture

- **Product:** Wayfound
- **Company/legal operator:** AutoTime AI Ltd.
- **Descriptor:** Evidence-backed decisions for cross-border technology careers.
- **Legal footer:** Wayfound is a product of AutoTime AI Ltd.
- **Coverage language:** Explore 32 European employment markets, with deeper
  evidence-governed guidance in selected countries.

Do not initially create multiple separately marketed sub-products. Existing
capabilities should remain parts of one coherent Wayfound experience:

- Decisions
- Countries and mobility
- Evidence
- Applications
- Interviews
- Outcomes

### 4.2 What does not change in the first rename

Unless separately approved, retain:

- the legal company name `AutoTime AI Ltd.`;
- the Git repository and local directory name;
- package names, database schemas, migration history and internal identifiers;
- stable API contracts, environment-variable names and analytics history;
- existing domains and deployment project identifiers until redirects and
  migration have been designed;
- historical decision records and audit evidence containing the former name.

This separation reduces risk and keeps a customer-facing brand decision from
becoming an unnecessary infrastructure migration.

## 5. Product positioning to test

### Primary category

Cross-border career decision support for technology professionals.

### Primary problem

International technology candidates waste time on jobs that are poorly
matched, operationally impractical, unsupported by their evidence, or unclear
about work rights and sponsorship.

### Differentiated promise

Wayfound helps a candidate decide whether a particular opportunity is worth
pursuing and shows the evidence, uncertainty and next verification step behind
that decision.

### Claims permitted before outcome validation

- Evidence-backed application decision support.
- Traceable reasons and identified evidence gaps.
- Country-aware investigation guidance.
- Application preparation grounded in candidate evidence.

### Claims not yet earned

- More interviews.
- Better hiring outcomes.
- Guaranteed job, sponsorship, visa or permit success.
- Complete immigration eligibility determination.
- Equal decision depth across all 32 markets.
- Trusted by users, unless supported by recorded user evidence.

## 6. Coverage language under the Wayfound brand

The brand must use one canonical coverage model:

| State | Meaning | Permitted output |
|---|---|---|
| Governed | Country-specific, versioned and reviewed evidence supports the decision path | Qualified country-specific decision support with limitations |
| Explorer | Recognised in-scope market without a complete governed country pack | Vacancy signals, missing evidence, official starting points and `Investigate first` |
| Ambiguous | The employing country cannot be established confidently | Ask the user to confirm the employing country |
| Outside coverage | Valid location outside the defined market scope | General job analysis only; no mobility conclusion |

The initial strategy is:

- 32 recognised European markets available for exploration;
- United Kingdom, Ireland, Germany and Netherlands as the current governed
  foundation, subject to production governance verification;
- eight additional priority markets progressively promoted only after their
  evidence, rules, tests and expert review pass the promotion gate.

## 7. Validation programme

No production rename should occur until Stages A-D are complete and the founder
records a GO decision.

### Stage A — collision and legal screen

Commission or perform a documented preliminary search covering:

- UK Intellectual Property Office;
- EUIPO;
- relevant WIPO records;
- UK Companies House;
- relevant software and business-name search results;
- Chrome Web Store, Apple and Google app stores where relevant;
- GitHub organisations and package registries;
- career, recruitment, HR, immigration, mobility and education products;
- `.com`, appropriate country domains and practical alternative domains;
- principal social handles.

Record exact matches, similar spellings, phonetic matches and related-category
uses. A clean domain is not evidence of trademark clearance. Obtain qualified
trademark advice before material public investment.

**Stop condition:** a material conflict in a relevant market or class that
cannot be reasonably mitigated.

### Stage B — linguistic and accessibility screen

Test at minimum with speakers or qualified reviewers for:

- English;
- German;
- French;
- Dutch;
- Spanish;
- Portuguese;
- one Nordic-language context;
- the founder's and initial users' principal first-language context.

Check:

- pronunciation on first sight;
- spelling after hearing it once;
- unintended meanings or offensive associations;
- confusion between `Wayfound`, `wayfinding`, `wayfinder` and `way found`;
- speech-to-text and screen-reader output;
- suitability in lower case, title case and a URL.

**Pass condition:** no serious negative meaning and at least 80% correct or
recognisably correct pronunciation/spelling in the small validation sample.

### Stage C — target-user comprehension and recall

Test with at least 5 target technology candidates. Prefer 8-10 if recruitment
is practical. Do not explain the intended meaning before the first questions.

Ask each participant:

1. What kind of product do you think `Wayfound` might be?
2. What feeling does the name create?
3. After seeing the descriptor, what do you believe the product does?
4. Does it sound credible for a product involving job and mobility evidence?
5. Does it sound like legal or immigration advice?
6. Can you recall and spell the name after a 15-minute unrelated task?
7. Would you feel comfortable sharing a Wayfound decision with a recruiter,
   adviser or colleague?

Measure:

- unaided recall;
- spelling accuracy;
- correct understanding after descriptor exposure;
- trust/credibility rating;
- perceived category;
- confusion with another known brand;
- qualitative objections.

The founder participates as the first user, but founder results must be logged
separately from external-user results.

### Stage D — proposition comparison

Compare, without manipulating participants toward the preferred answer:

- Wayfound;
- the current public name;
- one descriptive control name;
- one credible alternative retained after legal screening.

Use the same descriptor and product screenshot for each name. Randomise order.
Evaluate comprehension, recall, credibility, distinctiveness and willingness to
learn more. This is directional qualitative evidence, not statistically valid
market proof.

### Stage E — founder decision record

Create a dated decision record containing:

- evidence reviewed;
- legal-advice status;
- validation participants and anonymised results;
- scorecard;
- unresolved risks;
- chosen name and descriptor;
- GO, HOLD or NO-GO decision;
- founder signature/name and date.

## 8. Decision scorecard

Score each surviving name from 1-5. Do not compensate for a legal failure with
a high marketing score.

| Criterion | Weight | Minimum |
|---|---:|---:|
| Preliminary legal viability | Gate | Pass |
| Relevant digital identity availability | Gate | Pass |
| Target-user comprehension with descriptor | 15% | 4/5 |
| Unaided recall | 15% | 3/5 |
| Pronunciation and spelling | 10% | 4/5 |
| Credibility and trust fit | 15% | 4/5 |
| Distinctiveness/searchability | 15% | 4/5 |
| Fit with evidence-led decisions | 15% | 4/5 |
| Geographic and product extensibility | 10% | 4/5 |
| Solo-founder implementation practicality | 5% | 4/5 |

Recommended decision rule:

- **GO:** all gates pass, no unresolved severe risk, and weighted score >= 4.0.
- **HOLD:** no fatal issue, but material evidence or advice remains incomplete.
- **NO-GO:** legal conflict, harmful linguistic issue, persistent user confusion,
  or weighted score below 3.5.

Scores between 3.5 and 3.99 require explicit founder reasoning and a second
validation round before GO.

## 9. Implementation plan after GO

### Phase 0 — inventory, no writes

Claude Code should produce a complete occurrence and dependency inventory for:

- `AutoTime EU Apply`;
- `EU Apply`;
- `AutoTime` when it identifies the product rather than the company;
- public metadata, Open Graph and generated images;
- navigation, login, dashboard and extension UI;
- emails, billing, consent and legal documents;
- test fixtures, snapshots, videos and screenshots;
- analytics event properties and Sentry release/project references;
- domains, redirect assumptions and Content Security Policy;
- Chrome extension manifest and store material;
- support, privacy and contractual wording;
- external services and production configuration.

Classify every occurrence as:

- public product brand;
- legal company reference;
- internal technical identifier;
- historical/audit evidence;
- domain or infrastructure dependency;
- test-only fixture;
- generated artifact.

Do not use a global search-and-replace.

### Phase 1 — central brand configuration

Introduce one typed, canonical brand configuration consumed by public UI and
metadata. It should distinguish at least:

- product name;
- product descriptor;
- legal company name;
- support identity;
- canonical public URL;
- previous product name for transitional notices where required.

Add tests that prevent customer-facing product surfaces from diverging.

### Phase 2 — low-risk customer-facing transition

Update:

- landing page and metadata;
- login and authenticated navigation;
- product emails and standard transactional templates;
- product help and onboarding language;
- extension visible name and copy, subject to store requirements;
- pricing and checkout product display names;
- product documentation intended for customers.

Use `Wayfound, a product of AutoTime AI Ltd.` wherever the operator needs to be
clear. Do not obscure the legal contracting entity.

### Phase 3 — domain and external-service transition

Only after the target domain and certificates are controlled:

- configure the canonical domain;
- preserve the old domain with permanent redirects;
- preserve authentication callback compatibility;
- update email authentication and sending identity;
- update Stripe branding without changing product/price identifiers merely for
  cosmetic consistency;
- update OAuth, Supabase, Vercel, Sentry, analytics and extension origins;
- verify privacy, cookie and consent disclosures;
- monitor redirects, authentication and email delivery.

Do not retire the old domain until real traffic and authentication logs show the
transition is safe.

### Phase 4 — asset and store transition

- Create a small Wayfound design system before producing numerous assets.
- Retain the product's existing serious, evidence-led visual tone.
- Avoid travel clichés, flags, passports and government-like seals.
- Prefer a direction/path/checkpoint visual metaphor.
- Update favicon, social cards, demo media and extension/store assets.
- Re-record screenshots only after UI terminology stabilises.

### Phase 5 — verification and release

Required checks include:

- typecheck, focused unit tests and production build;
- landing, login, dashboard and core decision-flow browser verification;
- mobile and desktop visual checks;
- metadata and social-card verification;
- authentication and callback verification on both old and new domains;
- Stripe checkout and webhook identity verification;
- Resend delivery and sender verification;
- Sentry event/release verification;
- analytics continuity and consent verification;
- extension installed-name, origin and store validation;
- privacy, terms, support and company-identity review;
- link and redirect crawl;
- rollback rehearsal.

The release remains a controlled product-brand transition, not a legal company
rename.

## 10. Rollout and rollback

Prefer a staged transition:

1. Internal/dev: Wayfound candidate branding.
2. Founder test account: full journey validation.
3. Private beta: `Wayfound, formerly AutoTime EU Apply` for a limited period.
4. Public surfaces: Wayfound primary, AutoTime AI Ltd. as operator.
5. Remove former-name transition copy after users, links and integrations have
   demonstrably migrated.

Rollback must be configuration-led. Retain the former assets, canonical-domain
configuration and brand values until the observation period closes. Never
rewrite historical audit records simply to make them visually current.

## 11. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Existing `Wayfound` conflict | Legal and market screen before design investment |
| Users interpret it as travel/navigation | Always test and initially pair it with the category descriptor |
| The name overstates certainty | Preserve uncertainty, limitations and decision-support language |
| Founder preference biases tests | Use neutral scripts, randomised comparison and separate founder results |
| Code-wide rename breaks infrastructure | Separate public brand from internal identifiers and migrate in phases |
| Loss of existing recognition or links | Transitional former-name copy and durable redirects |
| Brand project distracts from validation | Time-box naming work and do not pause founder-led product UAT |
| Visual rebrand consumes excessive bootstrap budget | Start with typography, wordmark, colour and a minimal asset set |

## 12. Bootstrapper constraints

Wayfound must earn adoption without a large launch budget. Therefore:

- the descriptor must accompany the name until comprehension is demonstrated;
- do not commission a large brand system before legal and user gates pass;
- do not rename infrastructure for aesthetic completeness;
- reuse the existing product UI where it already communicates trust;
- prioritise landing page, core workflow, email and extension consistency;
- measure whether the new name improves recall and explanation in real founder
  conversations;
- continue validating willingness to pay—the new name cannot substitute for
  demand evidence.

Suggested minimum viable brand package after GO:

1. wordmark;
2. compact product mark/favicon;
3. primary and neutral colour tokens;
4. typography rules;
5. product descriptor and one-sentence explanation;
6. landing, app-shell, email and social-card applications;
7. correct legal attribution.

## 14. Definition of done

The naming project is complete only when:

- Wayfound passes the legal, linguistic and user-validation gates;
- a dated GO decision exists;
- customer-facing surfaces use one canonical brand configuration;
- the legal company remains correctly disclosed;
- domains, auth, email, payments, analytics, monitoring and extension flows are
  verified;
- old links and users have a safe transition path;
- core product claims remain accurate and evidence-backed;
- launch evidence and rollback instructions are recorded;
- no historical evidence or technical identity was unnecessarily rewritten.

Until those conditions are satisfied, `Wayfound` remains the preferred
candidate—not the production identity.

candidate against the product that exists now and the planned 32-market
strategy. It does not override the validation gates in this document.

### 15.2 What EU Apply communicates

Without additional context, `EU Apply` suggests:

- a tool for applying to jobs in European Union countries;
- application-form assistance or application automation;
- a transactional step near the end of the job-search process;
- broad but relatively shallow geographic functionality.

Those expectations are not completely wrong—the product includes application
preparation and targets European roles—but they omit its strongest qualities:

- deciding whether an opportunity deserves effort;
- distinguishing evidence from assumptions and unknowns;
- exposing work-right and sponsorship uncertainty;
- preserving traceability and provenance;
- learning from corrections and outcomes.

The name also creates a factual scope problem. A product covering the United
Kingdom, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein alongside the EU 27 is
European, but it is not limited to the European Union.

### 15.3 What Wayfound communicates

Without a descriptor, `Wayfound` suggests:

- a path has been discovered or clarified;
- movement from uncertainty toward direction;
- guidance rather than automatic execution;
- a human outcome rather than a software feature.

That territory aligns with the core product question:

> Is this opportunity worth pursuing, what evidence supports the decision, and
> what should the candidate verify or do next?

Wayfound does not explain the job-search category on its own. During the early
brand-building period it must always appear with a clear descriptor, such as:

> **Wayfound**  
> Evidence-backed decisions for cross-border technology careers.

For conversion-oriented surfaces, a more concrete supporting line can follow:

> Find the European technology opportunities worth your time—and understand
> the evidence before you apply.

### 15.4 Before-and-after positioning

| Current framing | Proposed framing |
|---|---|
| EU Apply | Wayfound |
| European application workspace | Cross-border career decision platform |
| Apply more efficiently | Invest effort in the right opportunities |
| Country-aware fit | Evidence-backed job and mobility decisions |
| EU Fit Engine | Opportunity decision with traceable evidence |
| Application workflow | Decision → verification → preparation → outcome learning |
| Tool identity | Product/company-quality brand identity |

The proposed framing must not erase application preparation. It changes the
hierarchy: preparation is an important capability that follows a decision, not
the product's entire identity.

### 15.5 Competitive interpretation

`EU Apply` places the product close to:

- autofill extensions;
- auto-application services;
- job-board application utilities;
- resume and cover-letter generators.

In that category, users are likely to compare breadth, speed, number of
supported sites and application volume. Those are not the strongest grounds on
which this product can compete.

`Wayfound` creates room to establish a different category:

- evidence-backed opportunity selection;
- cross-border career decision support;
- traceable job and mobility reasoning;
- quality over application volume.

A name alone does not create that category. The landing page, onboarding,
decision interface, pricing and founder-led sales explanation must reinforce
the same idea.

### 15.6 Bootstrapper trade-off

For a solo bootstrapper, the decision is not simply distinctive versus
generic. It is short-term explanation cost versus long-term ownership.

**EU Apply advantages:**

- already implemented across the product;
- immediately understandable at a basic level;
- no rebrand cost;
- usable during private validation without further work.

**EU Apply disadvantages:**

- difficult to own in search and memory;
- sets an inaccurate geographic expectation for 32 markets;
- encourages comparison with automation and autofill tools;
- will become more restrictive as the platform matures;
- may require a later rename after more users and integrations exist, when the
  transition would be more expensive.

**Wayfound advantages:**

- more distinctive and capable of accumulating independent brand equity;
- aligned with the decision-and-direction experience;
- works across countries and future regions;
- does not reduce the product to application submission;
- gives the founder a stronger story for quality over volume.

**Wayfound disadvantages:**

- needs a descriptor and repeated explanation initially;
- legal and linguistic availability are not yet established;
- creates implementation and migration work;
- could be mistaken for navigation, travel or consulting without consistent
  category language;
- cannot compensate for absent demand or outcome validation.

For the current early stage, this is the least costly period in which to test a
distinctive name—but only if testing is time-boxed and does not interrupt paid
pilot validation.

### 15.8 When retaining EU Apply would be rational

Retain the current name for the next validation period if:

- Wayfound has a material legal or market conflict;
- target users repeatedly fail to remember, spell or understand it even with
  the descriptor;
- the founder cannot consistently explain the connection between the name and
  product value;
- rebranding would delay the first paid pilot;
- the product is deliberately narrowed back to EU-only application assistance.

In that case, `EU Apply` should be treated as a temporary functional name while
another distinctive candidate is researched—not automatically as the final
long-term brand.

### 15.9 Working recommendation

The current evidence supports this provisional decision:

> Continue operating the existing product as EU Apply while validating
> Wayfound. If Wayfound clears the legal, linguistic and target-user gates,
> adopt it as the customer-facing product brand through the staged transition
> in this document. Do not rename the legal company or internal infrastructure
> merely to match the public brand.

This preserves present operating continuity while testing a name with greater
long-term fit and ownership potential.
