# Synthetic tech-candidate discovery rehearsal

**Date:** 17 September 2026  
**Status:** Twelve invented role-plays, not interviews, market research, testimonials, or demand evidence.  
**Purpose:** Stress-test the [slice-selection interview kit](slice-selection-interview-kit.md), anticipate distinct decision problems, and choose a *provisional recruitment hypothesis*. No real person, vacancy, payment, or product session is represented here.

## Method and limits

I simulated 12 plausible answers to the neutral discovery questions, spanning four role-family/corridor hypotheses and different work-right situations. Each row is an invented answer, not a quote. The simulations are deliberately varied, including people who would **not** pay. Their apparent frequency reflects how I constructed the sample and must not be read as market prevalence. I did not score willingness to pay from role-play answers: all paid-demand evidence is **zero**.

These are short role-plays, not fabricated verbatim interview transcripts. No actual person said these words.

## What the simulation usefully exposes

1. **Work-right segmentation is essential.** S04 and S07 would be harmed or alienated by applying a sponsorship-needed template to everyone.
2. **Entity and vacancy specificity matter.** S02, S05 and S11 illustrate why a sponsor-register match is not permission to promise sponsorship for a particular vacancy.
3. **Role fit must identify hard requirements, not only keywords.** S03, S10 and S12 expose different specialist-depth needs.
4. **The value proposition may be episodic.** S06 and S08 suggest testing per-decision or shortlist pricing as well as subscription pricing. That is a product hypothesis, not price evidence.
5. **Correct abstention may be the valuable output.** Several cases need “Investigate” with a specific verification question rather than a confident Apply/Skip verdict.

## Provisional first slice, not a validated choice

**Recruitment hypothesis:** sponsorship-required, mid/senior data engineers assessing direct-employer Irish vacancies. This is a narrow overlap of role fit, candidate evidence, entity identity and mobility uncertainty, and Ireland already has a dedicated country pack in the product. Exclude staffing-agency and graduate cases from the first safety/quality claim, but retain them as adversarial tests. The intended output is a defensible **Apply / Improve / Investigate / Skip** recommendation with evidence and explicit unresolved questions—not immigration eligibility or a hiring prediction.

The choice is provisional because the simulated “participants” provide no evidence of reachability, real pain frequency, actual vacancy volume, ability to maintain Irish sources, expert review, or payment. It should be rejected if real discovery interviews favour another slice.

### Questions that could overturn the hypothesis

- Can the founder reach 8–12 candidates across at least two role-family/corridor alternatives, then recruit 3–5 in the chosen slice?
- Do these candidates actually make repeated vacancy-level decisions, or is their problem mainly obtaining interviews after applying?
- Are direct-employer Irish data-engineering vacancies with relevant uncertainty plentiful enough for a 30–50-case corpus?
- Is the decisive pain mobility ambiguity, specialist fit, entity verification, or simply poor job-posting quality?
- Can a qualified reviewer sign off on any consequential Irish mobility statements, or must the pilot remain information-only?
- Does anyone make a priced commitment after seeing a real decision, rather than merely expressing interest?

## Decision and next evidence step

This exercise satisfies the requested **12-candidate role-play**, but **0/8–12 real discovery interviews**, **0/3–5 observed pilot participants**, **0 real purchase commitments**, and **0 independent expert labels**. The next legitimate evidence step is to run the neutral interviews in the kit, include alternatives to the provisional slice, and record an actual slice-selection memo before claiming validation.
