## Who to recruit

Tech professionals who are, or recently were, actively searching for a sponsored role in Europe. Realistic sourcing channels, cheapest/fastest first:
- Your own network — anyone who's mentioned visa/relocation job-hunting to you, even tangentially.
- r/cscareerquestionsEU, r/germany, r/ireland, r/AskUK — post asking for 15 minutes of someone's time in exchange for early access to the tool once it's ready.
- International student Discord/Facebook groups at universities with strong CS programs.
- LinkedIn: search "visa sponsorship" + a target role title, DM people who've posted about the search being hard.

Aim for spread across at least 2-3 of the role families already coded into the product (`software engineering`, `data engineering/analytics`, `cybersecurity`, `technical business analysis`, `cloud/platform engineering` — from `tech-fintech.ts`), not all from one.

## Recruitment message (copy-paste ready)

> Hi [name] — I'm building a tool to help tech professionals figure out which sponsored-visa roles in Europe are actually worth applying to, before they spend hours on an application. I'm talking to people who've actually done this search to make sure I'm solving the real problem, not a guessed one. Would you have 15-20 minutes for a call? Happy to share what I build with you first, no pitch, just genuinely want to understand what the search was like for you.

## Preferred neutral discovery script (15-20 minutes)

Ask for a specific recent episode before asking for opinions about a product.

1. What tech role and hiring country were you considering most recently? What was your work-right situation for that role?
2. Show me the last vacancy you seriously considered. What did you do next, and why?
3. What information was missing or uncertain? How did you try to verify it, and how long did that take?
4. Tell me about a vacancy you pursued and later regretted, or one you skipped and later reconsidered. What happened?
5. What tools, people or sources did you use? What did they cost in money or time?
6. What was the last job-search product or service you actually paid for? If none, what kept you from paying?
7. Which part of this decision would you most want help with? What would make you distrust an answer?
8. Only after these answers: describe a concrete LandWell decision example and a specific price. Ask whether they would make a priced commitment, use it for their next real vacancy, or decline. Record the action or refusal, not just a stated preference.

Avoid promising sponsorship eligibility, a job, or an interview. Do not treat a hypothetical “yes” as a sale.

## Earlier interview prompts (use only where relevant)

Ask in this order. Don't lead the witness — let them describe the problem before you describe the product.

1. **Walk me through the last time you searched for a job that needed visa sponsorship.** What did that actually look like, day to day?
2. **How did you decide which postings were worth applying to, versus skipping?** What information did you wish you had at that moment?
3. **Tell me about a time you applied to something and later found out it wasn't actually going to work** (sponsorship fell through, salary too low, wrong seniority, etc.). What would have told you that earlier?
4. **How much time would you say you spent on applications that went nowhere, specifically because of a mobility/visa issue you didn't know about upfront?**
5. **If a tool could tell you, before you apply, "here's what's known, here's what's missing, here's what to verify" — would that have changed what you applied to?** Be specific if you can.
6. **Would you have paid for that, and roughly how much?** (Don't accept "yes, definitely" at face value — ask what they'd compare it to, or what they currently pay for job-search tools, if anything.)
7. **What role were you searching for, and which countries?** (This is the data point that actually answers the slice-selection question — ask last, after they've talked freely.)

## Scoring sheet — fill in after each interview

**Use the full eight-criterion rubric from the parent plan**, not just the four
criteria in the quick table below: pain frequency, stakes, founder access,
evidence availability, decision tractability, differentiation, willingness
to pay, and maintenance cost. Score each 1–5 and cite the observed answer or
vacancy behind the score. A hypothetical purchase answer alone cannot score
high on willingness to pay. Group results by candidate situation **and** role
family **and** corridor; do not average unlike situations into one “tech” row.

## What "done" looks like

8-12 rows filled in, a clear pattern in one role-family + corridor combination scoring consistently higher than the rest, and the one-page hypothesis statement from §3 written out:

> For **[candidate situation]** evaluating **[role family]** vacancies in **[hiring-country corridor]**, LandWell helps them decide **[specific action]** by using **[specific evidence]**, reducing **[observable wasted effort or uncertainty]**.

That sentence, once you can write it from real interview evidence rather than a guess, is what unlocks step 3 (the real-vacancy case corpus) — which I can help build once the slice is picked, since finding and cataloguing real postings is something I can do directly.
